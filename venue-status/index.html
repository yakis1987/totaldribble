2030 World Cup venue status

Twenty-three stadiums were included in the hosting plans approved by FIFA in December 2024. Not all of them will host a match. One host city has already withdrawn, several venues do not yet exist, and FIFA is weighing a reduction in the overall number of grounds. This page records where each venue stands, what the source is, and when we last checked.

Status labels are our reading of publicly reported information, not FIFA designations. Where reporting is contested we mark the venue as at risk rather than removing it.

Method

How this table is maintained

Capacity figures are the most frequently mangled numbers in World Cup coverage, because three different values circulate for most grounds: the current all-seater capacity, the capacity after a planned renovation, and a historic figure that no longer applies. Where a stadium is being rebuilt we label the figure as planned. Where sources disagree we prefer the club or federation's own published number over aggregated listings.

Two specific cautions worth stating plainly. First, planned capacities are not commitments; several are attached to projects without confirmed funding or timelines. Second, figures announced by candidates for club office are proposals, not policy. A figure of 83,000 for Estadio da Luz circulated widely in late 2025 having come from a Benfica presidential candidate who subsequently lost the election. The figure carried by the club's actual leadership is 70,000.

What we do not know yet

The final venue is unannounced. The total number of host stadiums is unsettled, with reporting suggesting a cut from twenty to as few as seventeen across Spain, Portugal and Morocco. No ticket sale dates or prices have been published. Match allocations per venue have not been made. None of these gaps are filled anywhere on this site with an estimate presented as fact.
