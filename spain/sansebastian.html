Stadium

One Stadium, A Coastal Home

Like Bilbao, San Sebastián brings a single stadium to World Cup 2030 rather than two. Reale Arena is racing to expand ahead of the tournament, and its compact size only sharpens the atmosphere Real Sociedad's fans are known for.

Football Culture

La Real, The Basque Derby

Founded 1909 2 La Liga titles (1981, 1982) 191 Basque derbies since 1909 2020 Copa del Rey champions

⚪🔵 La Real

Real Sociedad de Fútbol, founded in 1909, is one of Spanish football's most respected academies, developing more homegrown talent than any other club in Europe's major leagues in a recent CIES Football Observatory study. The club followed a Basque-only signing policy of its own from around 1962 into the 1980s, but dropped it in 1989 when they signed Republic of Ireland striker John Aldridge, and extended this openness to non-Basque Spanish players in 2002.

Real Sociedad's golden era came in the early 1980s, winning back-to-back La Liga titles in 1981 and 1982 - part of a remarkable four-year stretch in which the Basque Country's two big clubs shared every league title between 1981 and 1984. More recently, La Real beat Athletic Bilbao in the 2020 Copa del Rey final, played behind closed doors in April 2021 due to the pandemic, and reached the Champions League for the first time in over a decade in 2023-24.

⚔️ The Basque Derby, From the Other Side

Real Sociedad's rivalry with Athletic Bilbao (covered in depth on our Bilbao guide) is one of football's oldest, first played in 1909. As of the 2024-25 season the sides had met 191 times in La Liga, Athletic winning 79 to Real Sociedad's 62 with 50 draws. Uniquely among major derbies, there's no fan segregation on matchday, and the two sets of supporters are known to mix freely.

That warmth traces back to a shared history under Franco: both clubs became vessels for suppressed Basque identity, most famously in December 1976 when the captains of Real Sociedad and Athletic Bilbao walked out together carrying the still-banned Ikurriña, the Basque flag, at Real Sociedad's old Atotxa ground - a gesture credited with helping the flag's re-legalisation the following year.

🏟 A Stadium Reborn Once Already

Reale Arena, long known simply as Anoeta, opened in 1993 with an athletics track separating fans from the pitch - a design fans criticised for years. The 2017-2019 renovation removed the track entirely, brought stands within touching distance of the game, and installed a retractable roof, transforming the atmosphere in the process. The next transformation, expanding capacity toward 42,247 seats, is already underway ahead of 2030.

A Regional Bid, Not Just a City Bid

There has been talk of the Basque Country presenting Reale Arena and Bilbao's San Mamés as a combined regional World Cup bid, sharing costs and infrastructure across the two Basque host cities roughly 100km apart.

Matchday

Where San Sebastián Watches Football

Parte Vieja's pintxos bars show every match on big screens for fans without tickets, and the streets around Reale Arena fill with txuri-urdin colours from early evening. Expect the loudest night of the tournament if a Basque derby against Athletic Bilbao falls on the calendar.

  • 🚶 Reale Arena under 30 min on foot from the Old Town
  • 🎉 Parte Vieja & the harbour - San Sebastián's natural gathering points
  • ⚔️ The Basque derby - 191 meetings with Athletic Bilbao since 1909

Matchday in San Sebastián

"There's no fan segregation at a Basque derby - opposing supporters are known to mix freely in the same stands, a rarity anywhere in football."

San Sebastián is compact enough that most visitors walk to Reale Arena from the centre, or catch one of several local bus lines. The build-up starts early in Parte Vieja, where pintxos bars fill with fans in Real Sociedad's blue and white well before kickoff.

For a Basque derby specifically, expect a genuinely different atmosphere from most Spanish football: both sets of fans have a long history of mutual respect rooted in shared regional identity, so the tension is real but rarely hostile.

Plaza de la Constitución in Parte Vieja and the La Concha promenade are San Sebastián's natural gathering points, likely candidates for an official fan zone, to be confirmed by FIFA closer to 2030.

Expect the walk back from Reale Arena to be busy for 20-30 minutes after a match. Many fans stop for a pintxo in Amara rather than join the crush straight away.

Where to Stay

Four Neighbourhoods, One Compact City

San Sebastián is small enough that almost anywhere central puts you within a short walk or bus ride of Reale Arena. Here's how the main districts compare.

  • 🕍 Parte Vieja - historic Old Town, pintxos capital, nightlife
  • 🏛 Centro - central, well-connected, easy walk to Reale Arena
  • 🏖 Gros - beach-side, surf culture, younger crowd
  • ⚽ Amara - the stadium's own neighbourhood, quiet and residential
🕍
Parte Vieja - Historic Old Town
San Sebastián's dense pintxos quarter, with over 100 bars packed into a few historic blocks. Central and lively, though busy and noisy at night in peak season.
🏛
Centro - Central & Well-Connected
Elegant Belle Époque streets between the Old Town and Reale Arena, with easy walking access to both the stadium and La Concha beach.
🏖
Gros - Beach & Surf Culture
Home to Zurriola beach and San Sebastián's surf scene, with a younger, more casual bar culture than Parte Vieja.
⚽
Amara - Matchday Convenience
The residential district immediately around Reale Arena. Limited hotel stock but genuinely walkable to kickoff if you're attending several matches.

Getting Around

A City Built for Walking

San Sebastián has no metro, but its compact size means most visitors barely need one. Local buses and the Euskotren narrow-gauge line cover the rest, and Reale Arena itself is walkable from most central hotels.

Between Matches

What to Do When There's No Match

San Sebastián rewards slow mornings on the beach and long pintxos evenings. Save a Michelin dinner for a night without an early match the next day.

🏖
La Concha Beach
Widely considered one of Europe's best urban beaches, a crescent of sand framed by the bay. A short walk from Parte Vieja and Centro.
🕍
Parte Vieja
San Sebastián's Old Town, home to over 100 pintxos bars packed into a compact grid of streets. The natural centre of the city's food culture.
⛰
Monte Igueldo
A funicular ride to a hilltop park with sweeping views over La Concha bay and the city below. A classic sunset spot.
🌊
Peine del Viento
Eduardo Chillida's iron sculptures set into the rocks at the western end of La Concha, where the waves crash dramatically against the coastline.

Food & Drink

What to Eat Before Kickoff

San Sebastián has the highest concentration of Michelin stars per capita anywhere in the world - roughly 19 stars within a short radius of the city, including three three-starred kitchens. Here's how the city eats at every budget.

  • 🍤 Pintxos - Parte Vieja's 100+ bars, the essential experience
  • 🦐 Gilda - the original pintxo, anchovy, olive and pickled pepper
  • 🐟 Kokotxas - hake or cod cheeks in a delicate green sauce
  • 🍾 Txakoli - the region's lightly sparkling white wine
  • ⭐ Arzak, Akelarre & Martín Berasategui - 3 three-Michelin-star kitchens

Here's what to order at each budget, from pre-match pintxos to the world's highest concentration of Michelin stars.

If time allows only one meal outside a match, make it pintxos in the Old Town rather than a rushed sit-down dinner.

Practical Info

Before You Go

Weather, safety, language and money - the basics every fan should have sorted before landing in San Sebastián.

  • 🌡️ Mild, often rainy summers - cooler than inland or southern Spain
  • 🛂 Schengen Area - visa-free for 60+ nationalities
  • 💶 €90-160/day comfortably covers hotel, food and transport
🌤

Weather

21-25°C in June-July, mild and often wet given San Sebastián's Atlantic coastal climate. Pack a light rain jacket even in midsummer.

🛡

Safety

San Sebastián is one of Spain's safer cities, with low rates of violent crime. Standard pickpocket precautions apply in busy parts of Parte Vieja and along La Concha in peak season.

🗣

Language

Basque (Euskara) and Spanish are both official; English is widely spoken in hotels, restaurants and tourism-facing venues.

💳

Money

Euro (€). Cards accepted almost everywhere, including pintxos bars. ATMs widely available.

FAQ

San Sebastián Questions

How do I get from San Sebastián to Reale Arena?
Reale Arena sits in the Amara district, about a 20-30 minute walk from the Old Town, or a short ride on local bus lines 17, 24, 28, 26 or 37. The Anoeta Euskotren station also sits right by the stadium complex.
What is Reale Arena's capacity for the World Cup?
Reale Arena currently holds around 40,000-40,177 seats. Real Sociedad has approved a further expansion to 42,247 seats at a cost of about 10.6 million euros, specifically to meet FIFA's requirement of roughly 40,000 seats plus press and hospitality capacity for the 2030 World Cup.
Why does San Sebastián only have one World Cup stadium?
Like Bilbao, San Sebastián is putting forward a single stadium, Reale Arena, rather than two. There has also been discussion of the Basque Country presenting San Mamés and Reale Arena as a combined regional bid to FIFA to share hosting costs and infrastructure.
Is Reale Arena a new stadium?
The stadium, long known as Anoeta, opened in 1993 and underwent a major renovation between 2017 and 2019 that removed the athletics track and brought stands closer to the pitch, raising capacity from around 32,000 to roughly 40,000 and dramatically improving the atmosphere.
How many World Cup 2030 matches will San Sebastián host?
Exact fixtures depend on FIFA's official match schedule, expected around 2027-2028. As a single-stadium city with one of the smaller capacities among Spain's venues, San Sebastián is expected to host a limited number of group-stage matches.
What is the best neighbourhood to stay in San Sebastián for the World Cup?
Parte Vieja (the Old Town) puts you in the heart of the pintxos scene and close to the harbour, though it gets busy and noisy at night. Centro and Amara offer a quieter base within easy reach of Reale Arena. Gros suits travellers who want beach access and a younger, surf-town atmosphere.
Should I stay near Reale Arena or in central San Sebastián?
Stay centrally. San Sebastián is compact enough that Parte Vieja, Centro or Gros all reach Reale Arena within a 20-35 minute walk or a short bus ride, while putting you close to the beaches and pintxos bars that make the city worth visiting beyond match days.
Is San Sebastián expensive compared to other World Cup 2030 host cities?
San Sebastián is one of the pricier Spanish host cities, reflecting its status as a top culinary and beach destination. Budget hotel rooms outside the tournament premium typically run €90-150/night, though pintxos bars remain accessible even on a modest budget.
What is the Basque derby?
The Basque derby (Euskal Derbia) is the rivalry between Real Sociedad and Athletic Bilbao, first played in 1909 and sometimes called the AP-8 derby after the motorway connecting the two cities. As of the 2024-25 season the sides had met 191 times in La Liga, Athletic winning 79 to Real Sociedad's 62 with 50 draws. Unlike many derbies, the two fan bases are known for a genuinely friendly relationship.
Does Real Sociedad have a Basque-only signing policy like Athletic Bilbao?
Not anymore. Real Sociedad followed a similar Basque-only policy from around 1962, but dropped it in 1989 when they signed Republic of Ireland forward John Aldridge, and extended this to non-Basque Spanish players in 2002. Athletic Bilbao is now the only major club still maintaining the restriction.
What is the Ikurriña derby?
In December 1976, a year after Franco's death, the captains of Real Sociedad and Athletic Bilbao walked onto the pitch at Real Sociedad's old Atotxa ground carrying the still technically-banned Ikurriña, the Basque flag. The gesture is credited with helping accelerate the flag's official re-legalisation in 1977 and remains one of Spanish football's most symbolic moments.
Has Real Sociedad won La Liga?
Yes, Real Sociedad won back-to-back La Liga titles in 1981 and 1982, part of a golden Basque era in which Real Sociedad and Athletic Bilbao shared four consecutive league titles between them from 1981 to 1984.
What is the weather like in San Sebastián in June and July?
Mild and often unpredictable. San Sebastián's Atlantic coastal climate means June and July afternoons typically reach 21-25°C, cooler and considerably wetter than inland or southern Spain. Pack a light rain jacket even in midsummer.
What should I eat in San Sebastián?
San Sebastián has the highest concentration of Michelin stars per capita anywhere in the world, with three three-Michelin-star restaurants: Arzak, Akelarre and Martín Berasategui. For a more affordable introduction, spend an evening pintxos-hopping through Parte Vieja's more than 100 bars, pairing bites with local txakoli wine or cider.
Is San Sebastián safe for tourists during the World Cup?
Yes, San Sebastián is considered one of Spain's safer cities, with low rates of violent crime. Standard pickpocket precautions apply in the busiest parts of Parte Vieja and along La Concha beach in peak season.
Can I day-trip from San Sebastián during the World Cup?
Yes. Bilbao is about 1 hour 15 minutes away by bus or 2.5 hours by the scenic Euskotren line, making a two-city Basque Country trip easy. Biarritz and the French Basque coast are also under an hour away. Avoid scheduling a day trip on a San Sebastián match day given kickoff-time crowds.
