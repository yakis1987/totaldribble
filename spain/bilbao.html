Stadium

One Stadium, A Century of Identity

Bilbao is one of only a few World Cup 2030 host cities with a single stadium rather than two - but San Mamés carries a weight of history most two-stadium cities can't match. Nicknamed "La Catedral" since 1913, it's the home of the only major European club that has never signed a player from outside the Basque Country.

Football Culture

The Cantera, The Cathedral

Founded 1898 Never relegated since 1929 191 Basque derbies since 1909 Cantera policy since 1912

🦁 The Lions of San Mamés

Athletic Club was founded in 1898 by Basque students returning from England and British industrial workers in Bilbao, which is why the club kept the English spelling "Athletic" rather than a Spanish translation. Since 1912, Athletic has followed an unwritten but unbreakable rule: it only signs players who were born in the Basque Country or developed at a Basque club. The motto - "Con cantera y afición, no hace falta importación" ("with home-grown talent and local support, there's no need for imports") - has never stopped Athletic staying in La Liga's top flight since 1929, a distinction shared only with Real Madrid and Barcelona.

The policy extends beyond the men born within Basque borders - players developed at a Basque club from a young age qualify too, which is how some players with no Basque ancestry, like Catalan-born Enric Saborit, have worn the shirt. Athletic supporters are Los Leones (the Lions), and the club is one of only four in Spain, alongside Real Madrid, Barcelona and Osasuna, owned by its members rather than run as a corporation.

⚔️ The Basque Derby

The Basque derby (Euskal Derbia) between Athletic Bilbao and Real Sociedad, first played in 1909, is one of Spanish football's oldest rivalries - and one of its warmest. As of the 2024-25 season the sides had met 191 times in La Liga, Athletic winning 79 to Real Sociedad's 62 with 50 draws. Unlike most derbies, there's no fan segregation on matchday, and opposing supporters are known to mix freely in the same stands.

The rivalry's most famous moment came in December 1976, a year after Franco's death, when the captains of both teams walked onto the pitch at Real Sociedad's old Atotxa ground carrying the Ikurriña, the Basque flag, still technically banned at the time. The gesture is widely credited with accelerating the flag's official re-legalisation in 1977 and remains one of the most symbolic moments in Spanish sport - two rival captains, one regional identity, transcending 90 minutes of football.

🏆 A Cathedral Old and New

The original San Mamés stood from 1913 to 2013, earning the nickname "La Catedral" for its imposing presence and near-religious devotion of its fans - fittingly, since Bilbao itself had no cathedral of its own for much of that time. It hosted Spain's first-ever international match in 1921 and several matches at the 1982 World Cup.

The new San Mamés, opened in 2013 on an adjacent plot, kept the nickname and much of the atmosphere while adding a UEFA Elite category rating. It hosted four Euro 2020 matches, including two of Spain's group games, and continues to welcome Champions League and Europa League nights - now with 2030 World Cup football to add to that list.

A Regional Bid, Not Just a City Bid

There has been talk of the Basque Country presenting San Mamés and San Sebastián's Reale Arena as a combined regional World Cup bid, sharing costs and infrastructure across the two Basque host cities roughly 100km apart.

Matchday

Where Bilbao Watches Football

Calle Licenciado Poza fills up on matchdays, an easy walk or short tram ride from San Mamés. Casco Viejo's pintxos bars show every match on big screens for fans without tickets. Expect the loudest night of the tournament if a Basque derby against Real Sociedad falls on Bilbao's calendar.

Matchday in Bilbao

"There's no fan segregation at a Basque derby - opposing supporters are known to mix freely in the same stands, a rarity anywhere in football."

Bilbao is a compact, walkable city, and San Mamés sits close enough to the centre that most visitors reach it on foot or with a single Metro or tram ride. The atmosphere builds steadily through the afternoon along Calle Licenciado Poza, Bilbao's main matchday bar strip, where fans in red-and-white gather for pintxos and drinks well before kickoff.

For a Basque derby specifically, expect a genuinely different atmosphere from most Spanish football: both sets of fans have a long history of mutual respect rooted in shared regional identity, so the tension is real but rarely hostile.

Pre-Match

Expect the San Mamés tram stop and nearby Metro station to be busy for 20-30 minutes after a match. Many fans linger at a Poza street bar rather than join the crush straight away.

Where to Stay

Four Neighbourhoods, One Compact City

Bilbao is small enough that almost anywhere central puts you within a short walk or tram ride of San Mamés. Here's how the main districts compare.

  • 🏛 Abando - central, well-connected, the default first choice
  • 🕍 Casco Viejo - historic Old Town, pintxos bars, nightlife
  • 🎨 Deusto & Guggenheim area - riverside, museums, quieter
  • ⚽ Indautxu - the stadium's own neighbourhood, closest walk to San Mamés
🏛
Abando - Central & Well-Connected
Bilbao's main train station district and commercial heart, with the widest hotel selection. A 15-20 minute walk or short tram ride to San Mamés.
🕍
Casco Viejo - Historic Old Town
The Siete Calles (Seven Streets) old quarter, Bilbao's own dense pintxos scene and liveliest nightlife. Central, atmospheric, a Metro or tram ride from the stadium.
🎨
Deusto & Guggenheim Area
Riverside district around Frank Gehry's titanium museum, quieter than Casco Viejo but still well connected by tram along the Ría.
⚽
Indautxu - Matchday Convenience
The district immediately around San Mamés itself. A genuine option here, unlike in bigger cities, since Bilbao's compact size means it's still close to everything else.

Getting Around

A City Built for Walking

Bilbao's Metro, tram and compact size mean most visitors barely need a taxi. The tram stops right by the Guggenheim and within walking distance of San Mamés; the Metro reaches the stadium in one or two stops from the centre.

Between Matches

What to Do When There's No Match

Bilbao rewards unhurried mornings along the Ría and long pintxos evenings. Save Gaztelugatxe for a genuine half-day out rather than squeezing it around a match schedule.

🎨
Guggenheim Museum
Frank Gehry's titanium-clad museum transformed Bilbao's global image after opening in 1997. Jeff Koons's flower-covered "Puppy" sculpture guards the entrance. Book timed entry online in peak season.
🕍
Casco Viejo
The Siete Calles (Seven Streets) old quarter, Bilbao's historic heart, packed with pintxos bars and small shops. A natural pre- or post-match wander.
🏛
Azkuna Zentroa
A former wine warehouse reimagined by Philippe Starck into a cultural centre with a cinema, library and a rooftop pool with a glass floor visible from below.
⛰
Gaztelugatxe
A dramatic islet chapel connected by a 241-step stone bridge, roughly 45 minutes from Bilbao by car. Familiar to Game of Thrones fans as a filming location - a genuine half-day trip, not a rushed stop.

Food & Drink

  • 🐟 Bacalao al pil-pil - salt cod in emulsified olive oil and garlic sauce
  • 🥩 Txuletón - thick grilled Basque beef chop
  • 🍾 Txakoli - the region's lightly sparkling white wine
  • ⭐ San Sebastián day trip - 3 three-Michelin-star restaurants within an hour

Practical Info

Before You Go

Weather, safety, language and money - the basics every fan should have sorted before landing in Bilbao.

  • 🌡️ Mild, often rainy summers - cooler than inland or southern Spain
  • 🛂 Schengen Area - visa-free for 60+ nationalities
  • 💶 €70-130/day comfortably covers hotel, food and transport
🌤

Weather

22-26°C in June-July, mild and often wet given Bilbao's Atlantic coastal climate. Pack a light rain jacket even in midsummer - it's noticeably cooler and wetter than Madrid or Seville.

🛡

Safety

Bilbao is one of Spain's safer major cities, with low rates of violent crime. Standard pickpocket precautions apply in Casco Viejo and around the Guggenheim.

🗣

Language

Basque (Euskara) and Spanish are both official; English is widely spoken in hotels, restaurants and tourism-facing venues across central Bilbao.

💳

Money

Euro (€). Cards accepted almost everywhere, including pintxos bars and Metro ticket machines. ATMs widely available.

As a single-stadium host city, Bilbao's hotel stock is smaller than Madrid's or Barcelona's, so rooms are likely to sell out earlier relative to demand around confirmed match dates. FAQ

Bilbao Questions

How do I get from Bilbao Airport to San Mamés?
Take the Bizkaibus A3247 shuttle from Bilbao Airport to the Bilbao Intermodal Station (Termibus/San Mamés area), a journey of about 20-30 minutes for around €3. From there, San Mamés is a short walk or one stop on the Bilbao Metro. A taxi from the airport takes about 20 minutes.
What is San Mamés's capacity for the World Cup?
San Mamés currently holds 53,331 seats. Athletic Bilbao has proposed an expansion to around 55,000-56,000 seats through internal seating optimisation rather than a full rebuild, partly to meet growing demand and partly to move closer to FIFA's preferred stadium sizes for 2030.
Why does Bilbao only have one World Cup stadium?
Unlike Madrid, Barcelona or Lisbon, which each have two host stadiums, Bilbao is putting forward San Mamés alone. There has also been discussion of the Basque Country presenting San Mamés and San Sebastián's Reale Arena as a combined regional bid to FIFA to share hosting costs and logistics.
Is San Mamés a new stadium?
The current San Mamés opened in 2013, built on an adjacent plot to the historic 1913 stadium it replaced, which was nicknamed "La Catedral" (The Cathedral). The new stadium kept the nickname and much of the atmosphere, and hosted matches at UEFA Euro 2020.
How many World Cup 2030 matches will Bilbao host?
Exact fixtures depend on FIFA's official match schedule, expected around 2027-2028. As a single-stadium city, Bilbao is expected to host fewer matches than two-stadium cities like Madrid, Barcelona or Lisbon, likely group-stage and early knockout fixtures.
What is the best neighbourhood to stay in Bilbao for the World Cup?
Abando and Indautxu offer the best combination of central location and proximity to San Mamés, both within a 15-20 minute walk or a couple of Metro stops. Casco Viejo (the Old Town) suits travellers who want historic atmosphere and nightlife over stadium proximity.
Should I stay near San Mamés or in central Bilbao?
Stay centrally. Bilbao is a compact city and San Mamés sits close to the Abando/Indautxu districts, so a central hotel puts you within easy walking or Metro distance of the stadium while keeping you close to the Guggenheim, Casco Viejo and the city's restaurants.
Is Bilbao expensive compared to other World Cup 2030 host cities?
Bilbao is generally cheaper than Madrid or Barcelona, with budget hotel rooms outside the tournament premium typically running €70-110/night. Pintxos bars make eating well affordable even on a tight budget.
What is the Basque derby?
The Basque derby (Euskal Derbia) is the rivalry between Athletic Bilbao and Real Sociedad, first played in 1909 and sometimes called the AP-8 derby after the motorway connecting the two cities. As of the 2024-25 season the sides had met 191 times in La Liga, with Bilbao winning 79 to Real Sociedad's 62 and 50 draws. Unlike many derbies, the two fan bases are known for a genuinely friendly relationship.
What is Athletic Bilbao's cantera policy?
Since 1912, Athletic Bilbao has fielded only players born in the Basque Country or developed at a Basque club - an unwritten but strictly followed rule known as the cantera policy. It makes Athletic unique among Europe's major clubs and has never prevented them from staying in La Liga's top division since 1929, alongside Real Madrid and Barcelona as the only ever-present clubs.
What is the Ikurriña derby?
In December 1976, a year after Franco's death, the captains of Athletic Bilbao and Real Sociedad walked onto the pitch at a Basque derby carrying the still technically-banned Ikurriña, the Basque flag. The gesture is credited with helping accelerate the flag's official re-legalisation in 1977 and remains one of Spanish football's most symbolic moments.
Is Athletic Bilbao connected to Atlético Madrid?
Historically, yes. Atlético Madrid was founded in 1903 as a branch club of Athletic Bilbao, initially called Athletic Club Sucursal de Madrid, before becoming fully independent in 1912. The clubs share the Anglicised "Athletic"/"Atlético" naming convention as a result, though they have no formal relationship today.
What is the weather like in Bilbao in June and July?
Mild and often unpredictable. Bilbao's Atlantic coastal climate means June and July afternoons typically reach 22-26°C, cooler and considerably wetter than inland Madrid or Seville. Pack a light rain jacket even in midsummer.
What should I eat in Bilbao?
Start with pintxos along Calle Ledesma or Casco Viejo's Siete Calles, Bilbao's answer to San Sebastián's more famous scene. Try bacalao al pil-pil, salt cod in an emulsified olive oil and garlic sauce, and txuletón, a thick grilled beef chop, a Basque speciality. Pair with a glass of txakoli, the region's lightly sparkling white wine.
Is Bilbao safe for tourists during the World Cup?
Yes, Bilbao is considered one of Spain's safer major cities, with low rates of violent crime and only the standard pickpocketing precautions needed in busy tourist areas like Casco Viejo and around the Guggenheim.
Can I day-trip from Bilbao during the World Cup?
Yes. San Sebastián is about 1 hour 15 minutes away by bus or 2.5 hours by the scenic Euskotren line. Gaztelugatxe, the dramatic islet chapel made famous by Game of Thrones, is roughly 45 minutes away by car. Avoid scheduling a day trip on a Bilbao match day given kickoff-time crowds.
