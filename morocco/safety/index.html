Is Morocco Safe?

The honest answer is that Morocco carries the same official risk rating as France, Spain and the United Kingdom. That single fact does more to settle the question than most of what is written about it. What follows is what the advisories actually say, where the real risks sit, and where the reporting on this subject is unreliable.

What The Advisories Say

Level 2, the same as Western Europe

The US State Department rates Morocco Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution, citing terrorism. The advisory notes that terrorist groups continue plotting possible attacks and may target tourist locations, transport hubs, markets, shopping malls and local government facilities.

That language sounds alarming in isolation. It reads differently once you know that France, Germany, Italy, Spain and the United Kingdom carry the identical Level 2 rating for the identical reason. This is a regional posture, not a Morocco-specific red flag.

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office does not advise against travel to any of Morocco's tourist destinations. Marrakech, Fez, Agadir, Chefchaouen, Essaouira, the Sahara and the Atlas Mountains are all open.

Source: US Department of State Morocco Travel Advisory, dated 21 April 2025, reissued after periodic review. Advisory levels change. Check the primary source before you travel rather than relying on this page or any other secondary summary.

A note on conflicting reporting

Several travel sites currently state that Morocco holds a Level 1 rating. We could not verify that against the State Department's own advisory page, which shows Level 2. Where a secondary source and a primary source disagree, we follow the primary source and say so. If the rating changes, this page will be updated with the date.

Solo Female Travel

More demanding, not off limits

This deserves a straight answer rather than reassurance. Morocco is harder for women travelling alone than most of Western Europe. Street harassment, mainly verbal and persistent rather than threatening, is widely and consistently reported, and it is most concentrated in the medinas of Marrakech and Fez.

Large numbers of women travel Morocco independently every year and have an excellent time. But it takes more active management than an equivalent trip to Lisbon or Seville, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

What consistently helps: dressing more conservatively than you would at home, particularly covering shoulders and knees; walking with visible purpose rather than pausing to look lost; booking a riad or hotel with a good reputation and using their recommended taxis; and avoiding narrow medina streets after dark.

Matchday 2030

What changes when the World Cup arrives

Morocco has already run the rehearsal. The country hosted the Africa Cup of Nations in 2025 across the same renovated stadiums proposed for 2030, which means the security and crowd management operations for large international football events have been tested rather than theorised.

Expect what you would expect at any major tournament: heavier security presence around venues, restricted road access on matchdays, and far larger crowds in medinas and transport hubs than the normal tourist season produces. The main practical consequence is that pickpocketing opportunities multiply, so the ordinary precautions matter more, not less.

Common Questions

Morocco safety questions

Is Morocco safe for tourists?

Broadly yes. The US State Department places Morocco at Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution, which is the same level applied to France, Germany, Italy, Spain and the United Kingdom. No government currently advises against travel to mainland Morocco's tourist circuit. The realistic risks are petty theft, persistent street selling and road safety rather than anything dramatic.

What does US State Department Level 2 actually mean?

It means exercise increased caution, and in Morocco's case the stated reason is the general possibility of terrorism at tourist sites, transport hubs and markets. It is a background awareness posture applied across the region, not a Morocco-specific warning. Several popular Western European countries carry the identical rating.

Is Morocco safe for solo female travellers?

It is more demanding than most of Western Europe. Street harassment is commonly reported, particularly in the medinas of Marrakech and Fez. Many women travel Morocco solo without incident, but it requires more active management than a solo trip to Lisbon or Madrid. Dressing more conservatively, avoiding empty streets after dark and booking accommodation with a reputable riad or hotel all reduce friction.

What is the main crime risk?

Petty theft. Pickpocketing and bag snatching in crowded medinas, transport hubs and beaches are the most frequently reported problems. Violent crime against tourists is not the primary concern. Standard precautions apply: keep your phone out of sight in crowds, do not carry everything in one place, and be firm and polite with unofficial guides.

Are there areas to avoid?

The Morocco to Algeria land border is closed, and a strip of Western Sahara near the militarised boundary line carries specific advisories. Neither is on any standard tourist or World Cup itinerary. All the 2030 host cities sit well away from both.

Is it safe to drive in Morocco?

Roads are the risk that gets understated. Mountain routes, notably between Marrakech and Ouarzazate, demand real caution, and winter ice affects the higher sections. If you are not confident, intercity trains and coaches are extensive and inexpensive.
