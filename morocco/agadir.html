Agadir

Morocco's leading Atlantic resort city, rebuilt entirely after the devastating 1960 earthquake, offers World Cup fans arguably the tournament's best climate. Stadium

One Stadium, Getting Its Roof

Agadir is putting forward a single World Cup venue. The Adrar Stadium's first renovation phase, focused on pitch, security and exterior works, wrapped up for AFCON 2025. A second phase, adding a full panoramic roof and removing the athletics track, is under way through 2030.

Open & Expanding
Adrar Stadium
Capacity currently around 42,000-45,000, rising to a FIFA-confirmed 46,000 gross (42,800 net) by 2030 · Also known as the Grand Stade d'Agadir, opened 11 October 2013 on a 32-hectare site near the Atlantic coast. Around 34 million euros invested in the first renovation phase alone, covering pitch replacement, security upgrades and expanded parking. The ongoing second phase adds a full 360-degree panoramic roof and removes the athletics track. Home to Botola Pro side Hassania Agadir.

Football Culture

Hassania Agadir, Voice of the Souss

HUSA 2019 Throne Cup finalists 2018-19 CAF Confederation Cup QF Ultras Imazighen

🔵 Hassania Union Sport d'Agadir

Hassania Agadir, universally known as HUSA, represents the Souss-Massa region with fierce local pride and moved into the newly-built Adrar Stadium in October 2013, replacing its old ground, Stade Al Inbiaat. The club has been a fixture of Morocco's top flight, the Botola Pro, in recent seasons, drawing on a passionate Amazigh fanbase across the wider Agadir region.

HUSA has never lifted a major domestic trophy but has come close: the club reached the 2019 Moroccan Throne Cup final. On the continental stage, Hassania qualified for the 2018-19 CAF Confederation Cup and advanced to the quarter-finals before losing to Egypt's Zamalek SC, then reached the semi-finals of the 2019-20 edition the following season, falling to fellow Moroccan side RS Berkane.

Attending a derby against Raja Casablanca or Wydad at Stade Adrar is one of the most atmospheric football experiences in Morocco.

- a description widely echoed in Moroccan football travel coverage

⚔️ Facing the Casablanca Giants

HUSA's biggest home fixtures come against Morocco's two most decorated clubs, Raja Casablanca and Wydad AC, matches that reliably fill the Adrar Stadium's stands. The club's dedicated supporters, Ultras Imazighen, are based in the stadium's atmospheric north and south end sections behind each goal.

Agadir's football identity is also inseparable from its Amazigh (Berber) heritage: Tashelhit is the majority first language across the region, and the Adrar Stadium's name itself, meaning "mountain" in Tashelhit, nods to the nearby Atlas foothills.

🏟️ A Stadium Under Transformation

Adrar Stadium's second renovation phase will remove the athletics track and add a full 360-degree roof, turning it from a multi-purpose venue into a purpose-built football bowl, elevating VIP areas in line with FIFA's requirements for 2030.

Matchday

Where Agadir Watches Football

The palm-lined Corniche and the cafes along Boulevard Mohammed V are Agadir's natural gathering points, a beach city that turns matchday into a genuinely relaxed, open-air occasion.

  • 🚗 Taxi - the main route from the beach hotel zone to the stadium, 15-20 minutes
  • 🎉 La Corniche & Marina Agadir - the city's natural gathering points
  • 🔵 Hassania Agadir - Souss-Massa's proud Botola Pro standard-bearer
🔵

Matchday in Agadir

Agadir turns football into a beach-town occasion unlike anywhere else Morocco hosts.

The Adrar Stadium sits about 6km east of the city centre and 8-10km from the main beach hotel zone, at the foot of the Atlas foothills, so most fans gather along the Corniche or in Marina Agadir before taking a taxi out to the ground. Expect a relaxed, sun-soaked pre-match atmosphere rather than the medina intensity of Fez or Marrakech.

For domestic HUSA fixtures against Raja or Wydad, the Adrar Stadium's north and south stands, home to Ultras Imazighen, provide some of the loudest, most sustained atmosphere anywhere in Morocco.

Where to Stay

Four Areas, One Long Beach

The Adrar Stadium sits inland and east of the city, away from the coast, so where you stay in Agadir comes down to beach access and atmosphere rather than stadium proximity.

  • 🏖️ La Corniche - the main beachfront hotel strip
  • ⛵ Marina Agadir - modern, upscale, yachts and boutiques
  • 🏢 Talborjt - the rebuilt city centre, practical and local
  • 🏜️ Founty - residential district near the Kasbah and marina
🏖️
La Corniche - Beachfront
Agadir's main tourist strip, a palm-lined promenade backed by resort hotels along a 10km stretch of golden sand. The most popular base for World Cup visitors.
⛵

A newer development at the foot of the Kasbah hill, with yachts, boutiques and waterfront restaurants. A stylish, walkable base close to Agadir Oufella.

Getting Around

Between Matches

What to Do When There's No Match

Agadir rewards a slower pace than Morocco's medina cities. Spend a morning at the Kasbah ruins, an afternoon on the beach, and an evening at the souk.

🏜️
Agadir Oufella (the Kasbah)
A 16th-century Saadian fortress on a hilltop above the city, largely destroyed by the 1960 earthquake and restored as a memorial site, with sweeping views over Agadir and the coast. Reachable by cable car.
🏪
Souk El Had
One of Morocco's largest markets, with roughly 3,000 stalls behind 15 gates, selling everything from spices and argan oil to leather goods and produce.
🌳
Jardin d'Olhao & Memory Museum
A tranquil Berber-style garden named for Agadir's sister city in Portugal, home to a small museum reflecting on the 1960 earthquake and the city's rebirth.
🏃
Taghazout & Paradise Valley
A laid-back surf town about 20km north of Agadir, plus the palm-shaded pools of Paradise Valley inland, both popular day trips from the city.

Food & Drink

What to Eat Before Kickoff

Agadir has long been one of Morocco's leading fishing ports, and the Souss-Massa region is the heart of the country's argan oil production, both show up on every menu.

  • 🐟 Fresh grilled fish & seafood - straight from Agadir's working port
  • 🍳 Tagines with argan oil - a distinctive Souss-Massa touch
  • 🍉 Amlou - almond, argan oil and honey dip, a regional specialty
  • 🍉 Fresh orange & prickly pear juice along the Corniche
  • ☕ Beachfront cafe culture along Boulevard Mohammed V

Here's what to order at each budget, from Souk El Had food stalls to marina-side seafood restaurants in Morocco's leading fishing port.

For an authentic, affordable meal, head to the fish stalls near the port rather than the more tourist-facing beachfront spots.

Practical Info

Before You Go

Weather, safety, language and money - the basics every fan should have sorted before landing in Agadir.

  • 🌡️ Morocco's mildest host-city climate, roughly 340 sunny days a year
  • 🚳 Visa-free for many nationalities for stays up to 90 days
  • 💵 $55-120/day comfortably covers hotel, food and transport
🌤

Weather

25-28C in June-July, arguably Morocco's most pleasant summer climate thanks to the Atlantic's cooling effect. Rain is almost entirely confined to winter months, and summers stay comfortable rather than scorching.

🛡

Safety

Agadir is Morocco's most established resort city and is generally very safe, with a modern layout and infrastructure built specifically for international tourism since its 1960s reconstruction.

🗣

Language

Tashelhit (Amazigh/Berber) is the majority first language locally, alongside Darija Arabic as a lingua franca. French is common in business, and English is widely spoken in the tourism sector.

💳

Money

Moroccan Dirham (MAD). Cash is preferred at souks and for petit taxis; cards are widely accepted at hotels, resorts and larger restaurants given Agadir's tourism-driven economy. ATMs are plentiful along the Corniche and in Talborjt.

Agadir already has Morocco's largest concentration of resort hotels, but World Cup demand will still be high along the beachfront, so book early around confirmed match dates. FAQ

Agadir Questions

Is the Adrar Stadium ready for the World Cup?
Partly. A first renovation phase, covering pitch, dressing rooms, security and exterior works, wrapped up for the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. A second phase, adding a 360-degree roof and removing the athletics track, is under way and targeted for completion before 2030.
What is the capacity of the Adrar Stadium?
Around 42,000-45,000 seats currently, with FIFA's confirmed figure for the 2030 World Cup set at a gross capacity of 46,000 (net 42,800) once the second renovation phase, including the new roof, is complete.
What makes the Adrar Stadium unique?
Its coastal setting at the foot of the Atlas Mountains, just a few kilometres from the Atlantic. The second renovation phase will add a full 360-degree panoramic roof and remove the athletics track, turning it into a purpose-built football bowl.
Has the Adrar Stadium already hosted matches?
Yes. It opened on 11 October 2013 with a friendly between Hassania Agadir and Algeria's JS Kabylie, hosted matches at the 2013 FIFA Club World Cup, and staged games during the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations.
How much has the Adrar Stadium renovation cost?
Around 34 million euros (about 39.5 million dollars) invested so far as part of the first renovation phase, with further spending planned for the second phase's roof and capacity expansion ahead of 2030.
What is the best neighbourhood to stay in Agadir for the World Cup?
The beachfront strip along the Corniche puts you closest to the sea and Agadir's main tourist infrastructure. Marina Agadir offers a more modern, upscale base, while Talborjt, the rebuilt city centre, is a practical option with easy access to transport.
Is Agadir expensive compared to other World Cup 2030 host cities?
Agadir is a major resort destination, so beachfront hotels can run higher than inland cities, but overall it remains reasonably affordable. A comfortable mid-range daily budget runs roughly $55-120, similar to Fez and Rabat.
Should I stay near the stadium or near the beach?
Stay near the beach. The Adrar Stadium sits about 6km east of the city centre and 8-10km from the main beach hotel zone, at the foot of the Atlas foothills, so most fans base themselves along the Corniche and take a taxi out on matchday.
What football club plays at the Adrar Stadium?
Hassania Union Sport d'Agadir (HUSA), commonly known as Hassania Agadir, the city's leading club, competing in the Botola Pro since moving into the stadium in 2013 from its former ground, Stade Al Inbiaat.
Has Hassania Agadir won any major trophies?
Hassania Agadir has not yet won a major domestic trophy, but reached the 2019 Moroccan Throne Cup final. The club also progressed to the CAF Confederation Cup quarter-finals in 2018-19 and the semi-finals of the 2019-20 edition.
Is there a big football rivalry in Agadir?
Hassania Agadir's biggest matches come against Morocco's Casablanca giants, Raja and Wydad, both of which reliably fill the Adrar Stadium. The club's Ultras Imazighen supporters group, based in the stadium's north and south end stands, is known as one of the most passionate in Morocco.
Is Agadir an Amazigh (Berber) city?
Yes, more so than most Moroccan host cities. Tashelhit, an Amazigh (Berber) language, is the majority first language in Agadir, and local identity, food and culture carry a strong Amazigh imprint alongside Darija Arabic and French.
What is the weather like in Agadir in June and July?
Agadir has arguably the best climate of any World Cup 2030 host city: mild, sunny and dry, with June-July highs typically around 25-28C and cooler evenings thanks to the Atlantic, without the intense heat of Marrakech or Fez.
What should I eat in Agadir?
Fresh seafood is the highlight, Agadir has long been one of Morocco's leading fishing ports. Also look for tagines cooked with argan oil, a Souss-Massa regional specialty, and fresh orange and prickly pear juices sold along the Corniche.
Is Agadir safe for tourists during the World Cup?
Yes, Agadir is Morocco's most established resort city and is generally very safe and well set up for international tourists, with a modern, purpose-built layout that dates from its post-1960 reconstruction.
What language is spoken in Agadir?
Tashelhit, an Amazigh (Berber) language, is the first language for most of the local population, alongside Darija Arabic as a lingua franca. French is common in business, and English is widely spoken in the tourism sector.
