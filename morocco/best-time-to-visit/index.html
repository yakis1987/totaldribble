Best Time to Visit Morocco

Spring and autumn, if you have a free choice. But most people reading this in the run-up to 2030 will not have a free choice, because the World Cup runs from 8 June to 21 July. So this guide covers both: the genuinely best months, and what the tournament window actually feels like city by city.

The Short Answer

March to May, September to November

Morocco's climate splits sharply between coast and interior, and that split matters more than the season. Marrakech is subtropical semi-desert with mild winters and very hot summers. The Atlantic coast is moderated by the cool Canary Current, which is why Agadir and Essaouira stay comfortable when Marrakech is punishing.

Spring gives you warmth without the extremes, and the rains thin out as it progresses. Autumn does the same in reverse. Both are good for combining cities, mountains and desert in one trip, which the summer effectively rules out.

What this means for 2030

Four of the six proposed Moroccan venues are coastal: Casablanca, Rabat, Tangier and Agadir. All four sit between 28C and 31C in July. The two inland venues, Marrakech and Fez, sit at 35C to 36C. If you are choosing between Moroccan host cities on comfort alone, the coast wins decisively. Note also that Fez is the Moroccan venue most often named in reporting as at risk of being cut, which is tracked on our venue status page.

Practical Consequences

How the heat changes a day in the city

In June and July inland, the working pattern is early and late. Sightseeing before roughly eleven in the morning and after five in the afternoon, with the middle of the day spent in shade, a riad courtyard or air conditioning. This is how Moroccans structure summer days and it is not optional advice.

Desert travel in July is genuinely ill-advised, with Merzouga averaging 43C. If the Sahara is on your list, it belongs to a different trip or a different month.

The coast is the release valve. Agadir at 31C with an Atlantic breeze is a completely different experience from Marrakech at 36C, and it is roughly three hours away by road. For anyone building a 2030 itinerary around Moroccan matches, basing on the coast and travelling inland for specific fixtures is worth considering.

Common Questions

When to visit questions

When is the best time to visit Morocco?

March to May and September to November. Both avoid the inland summer heat and the winter cold in the mountains, and both give you comfortable conditions for the medinas, the Atlas and the desert. April and October are the safest single-month picks.

How hot does Marrakech get in summer?

July is the peak, with average daytime highs around 36C. August is barely cooler at about 35C. Nights drop to roughly 20C. Marrakech sits at 460 metres and well inland, so the day to night swing is large.

Is Morocco too hot for the 2030 World Cup in June and July?

Inland, it is a genuine issue. Marrakech averages 36C in July and Fez around 35C. The coast is a different story: Casablanca and Rabat sit near 28C, Tangier around 29C and Agadir about 31C. Four of the six proposed Moroccan venues are coastal, which materially changes the picture.

Which is the coolest Moroccan host city in summer?

Casablanca and Rabat, both averaging around 28C in July. The Atlantic and the Canary Current keep the coast far more comfortable than anywhere inland.

When does it rain in Morocco?

Rain is concentrated between October and May and is scarce elsewhere. Marrakech receives about 344mm a year, with March the wettest month at around 50mm and July the driest at about 3mm. Agadir gets roughly 271mm annually, with December wettest.

Is winter a bad time to visit?

Not for the coast or the cities. Agadir still reaches about 20C in January. It is harder in the Atlas Mountains, where it can be cold with snow at higher elevations, and desert nights get genuinely cold even outside midwinter.
