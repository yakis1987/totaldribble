Architecture & Design

Designed Around Light

The current Estádio da Luz opened on 25 October 2003, replacing the original stadium of the same name that had stood since 1954 and once held 120,000 fans, the largest in Europe at its peak. Architect Damon Lavelle of HOK Sport Venue Event, the firm now known as Populous, designed the new stadium with a deliberate focus on light and transparency, reflected in the name Luz itself, taken from the Estrada da Luz on which the stadium sits.

Construction cost €160 million, with €22.6 million supported by the Portuguese government ahead of Euro 2004. The result became a UEFA category four stadium known for its elegantly curved stands and distinctive roof-supporting arches, elected the most beautiful stadium in Europe in a 2014 online poll by French sports newspaper L'Équipe.

By its fifteenth anniversary, the stadium had welcomed more than 17 million spectators, a testament to both Benfica's huge fanbase and the venue's role hosting some of European football's biggest occasions.

The Benfica District

A €220 Million Reimagining

On 22 July 2025, Benfica unveiled "Benfica District," a sweeping €220 million masterplan designed once again by Populous, this time working with Lisbon-based firm Saraiva + Associados. Rather than simply expanding the stadium, the plan reimagines the entire precinct around it as a year-round sports, culture and entertainment destination, explicitly benchmarked against Wembley Stadium and Manchester City's Etihad Campus.

The masterplan also adds two indoor sports halls (2,500 and 1,500 seats), a community swimming pool, a rooftop football pitch with running track, a 500-seat theatre, and a new fourth level with 6,800 square metres of mixed-use space for offices, hospitality and commercial facilities. The club's iconic Eusébio statue will be relocated to the new main entrance plaza.

What We Don't Know Yet

Reported timelines vary: construction could begin as early as June 2027 with a two-year build, though the club has also described a longer four-year horizon. A separate, more radical proposal for an entirely new 120,000-seat stadium, championed by a former club president, could also resurface depending on the outcome of internal club elections. We'll update this page once the final direction and timeline are confirmed.

History & Significance

Two Champions League Finals, One Euro Final

Estádio da Luz has hosted the Euro 2004 final and three group matches plus a quarter-final, as well as two UEFA Champions League finals: the 2013-14 final, in which Real Madrid beat rivals Atlético Madrid 4-1 after extra time, and the pandemic-relocated 2019-20 final, Bayern Munich's 1-0 win over Paris Saint-Germain. Between 2019 and 2024, the stadium hosted concerts by Ed Sheeran, Rammstein and Taylor Swift.

Estádio da Luz is one of three confirmed Portuguese venues for the 2030 World Cup, alongside Estádio José Alvalade, its Lisbon rival just 3km away, and Estádio do Dragão in Porto.

The stadium is the home of Lisbon's SL Benfica, Portugal's most decorated football club. On 17 February 2026, a Benfica-Real Madrid match set a new stadium attendance record with 66,387 spectators.

World Cup 2030 Role

Portugal's Biggest Stage

As Portugal's largest stadium and the country's most internationally recognised football venue, Estádio da Luz has already been earmarked to host a 2030 World Cup semi-final by the Portuguese Football Federation. The Benfica District masterplan, timed for completion around the 2029 UEFA Women's Euro and the 2030 World Cup, is designed specifically to secure that role and push the stadium's profile even further onto the global stage.

With Populous, the stadium's original architect, leading the redevelopment and drawing direct comparisons to Wembley and the Etihad Campus, Benfica's ambitions extend well beyond simply meeting FIFA's minimum requirements.

Getting There

On the Blue Line

Estádio da Luz is served directly by the Colégio Militar/Luz Metro station on Lisbon's Blue Line, dropping fans at the stadium's gates just a few minutes' walk away, a genuinely convenient central location.

Frequently Asked

Estádio da Luz Questions

Who designed Estádio da Luz?
Architect Damon Lavelle of HOK Sport Venue Event, now known as Populous, designed the stadium that opened in 2003, with a focus on light and transparency.
Why is it called "Estádio da Luz"?
The name comes from Estrada da Luz, the road on which the stadium sits, itself named after a nearby church, Igreja de Nossa Senhora da Luz. The common English translation "Stadium of Light" is a popular but not entirely literal rendering.
How much did the current stadium cost to build?
€160 million, with €22.6 million supported by the Portuguese government ahead of Euro 2004.
Has the stadium won any design awards?
Yes, it was voted the most beautiful stadium in Europe in a 2014 online poll conducted by French sports newspaper L'Équipe.
What is the current capacity of Estádio da Luz?
68,100 seats, Portugal's largest stadium.
What is the "Benfica District" project?
A €220 million masterplan unveiled in July 2025 by Populous and Saraiva + Associados, expanding the stadium's capacity and adding a 10,000-seat arena, sports halls, a swimming pool, a fan plaza and mixed-use commercial space around the venue.
How big could the stadium eventually become?
The current plan targets over 70,000 seats, with Populous architect Tom Jones saying the team is exploring options to increase capacity up to 80,000.
When will the redevelopment be complete?
Reports vary, with construction potentially starting in June 2027 and taking two to four years. Benfica hopes at least the stadium itself will be ready in time for the 2030 World Cup, and ideally the 2029 Women's Euro.
When did Estádio da Luz open?
25 October 2003, with Benfica beating Uruguay's Nacional 2-1 in an exhibition match, Nuno Gomes scoring both goals.
Has Estádio da Luz hosted Champions League finals?
Yes, twice: the 2013-14 final (Real Madrid 4-1 Atlético Madrid after extra time) and the pandemic-relocated 2019-20 final (Bayern Munich 1-0 Paris Saint-Germain).
What club plays at Estádio da Luz?
SL Benfica, Portugal's most decorated football club, has called the stadium home since 2003.
What is the stadium's attendance record?
66,387 spectators, set during a Benfica-Real Madrid match on 17 February 2026.
How do I get to Estádio da Luz?
The Colégio Militar/Luz Metro station on the Blue Line drops fans directly at the stadium's gates, roughly 15-20 minutes from central Lisbon.
What's the nearest airport to Estádio da Luz?
Lisbon Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS), roughly 30-35 minutes from the stadium via Metro and taxi.
Where should I stay for a match at Estádio da Luz?
Anywhere in central Lisbon works well thanks to the stadium's own Metro stop. See our full Lisbon guide for specific neighbourhood recommendations.
How close is Estádio José Alvalade?
Roughly 3km away, home to Lisbon rivals Sporting CP, making a two-stadium trip within the city entirely feasible for fans.
