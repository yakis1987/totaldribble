Architecture & Design

A Coastal Bowl,
Named for the Mountains Behind It

Adrar Stadium, also known as the Grand Stade d'Agadir, opened on 11 October 2013 on a 32-hectare site a few kilometres from Agadir's Atlantic coastline. Its name comes from Tashelhit, the Amazigh (Berber) language spoken across the Souss-Massa region, in which "adrar" simply means "mountain," a nod to the nearby Atlas foothills that give the stadium its backdrop.

Since replacing Hassania Agadir's older Stade Al Inbiaat as the club's home ground, the stadium has functioned as a genuine multi-purpose venue, hosting football alongside continental competition and civic events, reflecting Agadir's status as Morocco's leading Atlantic resort city and a major fishing port.

The current renovation, part of the same national programme reshaping Tangier, Marrakech, Rabat and Fez, is being carried out in two phases: the first, completed for AFCON 2025, focused on pitch, security and exterior upgrades, while the second and more ambitious phase will add a full panoramic roof and remove the stadium's athletics track entirely.

Adrar Stadium sits within a wider sports complex on Agadir's eastern edge, sharing its site with training pitches and supporting facilities used by Hassania Agadir's academy sides. The stadium's original design kept the running track in place around the pitch, a common approach for Moroccan venues built with an eye toward hosting athletics meets and continental multi-sport events alongside football, though it's the very feature Phase 2 will finally remove to bring fans closer to the action.

That two-phase approach, quick security and comfort upgrades first, structural transformation second, mirrors what's happening at several of Morocco's other 2030 venues. Fez Grand Stadium followed a similar rhythm, though it removed its own athletics track in Phase 1 rather than waiting for Phase 2, while Ibn Batouta Stadium in Tangier took the more ambitious route of a single, comprehensive rebuild that added a hybrid pitch and a full roof in one continuous project. Each city's choice reflects a different balance between AFCON 2025's tighter deadline and the longer runway available before 2030.

The "Sports City" model, a single large complex combining the main stadium with training pitches, academy facilities and supporting infrastructure, has become something of a template across Morocco's renovated venues, also visible at Ibn Batouta's own 74-hectare site in Tangier. For Adrar, that means visitors arriving on matchday will find more than just a stadium: youth pitches, administrative buildings and open plazas designed to keep the site active well beyond the 90 minutes of a football match, part of the broader civic ambition behind Morocco's World Cup infrastructure push.

Renovation & Fan Experience

A Roof, Then a Football-Only Bowl

Adrar Stadium's first renovation phase focused on the essentials needed to host AFCON 2025: pitch replacement, dressing room upgrades, improved security, and exterior works, all completed on schedule with roughly €34 million (about $39.5 million) invested.

That phase left the stadium's athletics track in place and its capacity largely unchanged. Behind the scenes, though, the upgrade also touched the fan-facing side of the stadium: renovated concourses, refreshed food and drink kiosks, and improved signage throughout the tunnels connecting the gates to the seating bowl, small changes that make a real difference on a packed matchday but rarely make headlines the way a new roof does.

Matchday Atmosphere

Red, White and Amazigh Pride

Hassania Agadir plays in red and white, chosen by the club's 1946 founders specifically to express Moroccan identity during the French protectorate era.

Search Agadir Hotels

That identity runs deeper than the kit: Agadir sits in the heart of Morocco's Amazigh (Berber) region, and the club's two main ultras groups, Ultras Imazighen (founded 2006) and Red Rebels (founded 2011), channel that heritage directly into matchday, staging tifos and choreography built around Amazigh symbolism rather than generic football imagery.

The result is a rally cry heard often around Adrar Stadium: "Dima Hassania, Dima Imazighen", roughly "Forever Hassania, forever Amazigh." Arrive an hour or two before kickoff and the streets around the ground fill with scarf and flag vendors, tea stalls, and fans making the walk in from Agadir's surrounding neighbourhoods, a pre-match ritual as much a part of the experience as the game itself.

Street food follows the same rhythm: grilled sandwiches, msemen (Moroccan flatbread) folded and sold hot off portable griddles, and glasses of sweet mint tea poured from tall kettles held high above the cup, a small piece of Moroccan hospitality that shows up outside stadiums the same way it does everywhere else in the country. Inside, the concourse snack bars sell the same fare at fixed prices, so there's no need to rush a purchase outside if you'd rather get to your seat early.

History & Significance

A Decade of Hassania Agadir

Adrar Stadium opened on 11 October 2013 with a friendly between Hassania Agadir and Algeria's JS Kabylie, immediately becoming the home of HUSA in place of the club's older ground, Stade Al Inbiaat.

Full Agadir Guide

Within its first year, the stadium was hosting continental and global fixtures, including matches at the 2013 FIFA Club World Cup. Adrar Stadium is one of six Moroccan venues being built or renovated for 2030, alongside Hassan II Stadium in Casablanca and Grand Stade de Marrakech. Outside major tournaments, it remains Hassania Agadir's regular home for Botola Pro fixtures throughout the domestic season.

The stadium is the home of Agadir's Hassania Union Sport d'Agadir (HUSA). The club has not yet won a major domestic trophy but reached the 2019 Moroccan Throne Cup final and advanced to the quarter-finals of the 2018-19 CAF Confederation Cup, before reaching the semi-finals of the 2019-20 edition the following season. HUSA's biggest fixtures come against Morocco's Casablanca giants, Raja and Wydad, both of which reliably fill the stadium to capacity on matchday.

World Cup 2030 Role

Morocco's Sunniest Venue

Agadir's Atlantic coastal climate is widely considered the mildest and most consistent of any Morocco 2030 host city, an asset the tournament's organisers will lean on for a stadium at the southern end of Morocco's six-city footprint, roughly 1,200km from Tangier in the north.

That distance also makes Agadir a logistically distinct venue, more isolated from Morocco's other host cities than any other, a genuine consideration for fans planning multi-city trips. Road trips to Marrakech take roughly three hours, while reaching Tangier in the north involves a full day of driving or a domestic flight, options worth weighing carefully against overland travel when following the tournament across multiple cities.

Once its Phase 2 renovation completes, Adrar Stadium's gross capacity of 46,000 (FIFA-confirmed, net 42,800) places it comfortably within FIFA's requirements for group-stage and knockout matches, continuing the stadium's run of hosting major tournament football that began with the 2013 FIFA Club World Cup, a track record that should reassure fans planning a trip built around this specific venue. Its capacity is modest next to the tournament's largest venues, Spain's Santiago Bernabéu and Camp Nou, both leading candidates to host the final, but that's typical for a venue expected to handle earlier-round matches rather than marquee occasions. It's closer in scale to Portugal's Estádio José Alvalade and Estádio do Dragão, both similarly positioned as solid mid-tier venues rather than marquee showpieces, a useful benchmark for fans planning a trip that spans all three host nations.

Getting There

From La Corniche to the Stadium

Adrar Stadium sits about 6km east of Agadir's city centre and roughly 8-10km from the main beach hotel zone along La Corniche, at the foot of the Atlas foothills. Taxis are the most straightforward way to reach the ground, widely available and inexpensive by international standards, while local bus routes also connect the stadium area to central Agadir for a fraction of the cost.

Where to Stay

Where to Stay for Adrar Stadium

We recommend basing yourself along the beach or in central Agadir rather than near the stadium itself, since the area around Adrar Stadium is a sports complex rather than a neighbourhood with hotels or restaurants. The beachfront La Corniche strip and central Talborjt both put you within easy taxi reach of the ground while keeping you close to the city's best dining and nightlife. Frequently Asked

Adrar Stadium Questions

What does "Adrar" mean?
"Adrar" means "mountain" in Tashelhit, the Amazigh (Berber) language spoken across Agadir's Souss-Massa region, a reference to the nearby Atlas foothills visible from the stadium.
What is the stadium's official name?
Officially the Grand Stade d'Agadir, though it's commonly known as Adrar Stadium.
Where is Adrar Stadium located?
On a 32-hectare site about 6km east of central Agadir, a few kilometres from the Atlantic coastline, at the foot of the Atlas foothills.
What stadium did Adrar Stadium replace?
Stade Al Inbiaat, Hassania Agadir's previous home ground, before the club moved to Adrar Stadium when it opened in October 2013.
What is the capacity of Adrar Stadium?
Around 42,000-45,000 seats currently, with FIFA's confirmed figure for the 2030 World Cup set at a gross capacity of 46,000 (net 42,800) once the second renovation phase, including the new roof, is complete.
Is Adrar Stadium ready for the World Cup?
Partly. Phase 1, covering pitch, security and exterior works, is complete and was used for AFCON 2025. Phase 2, adding a 360-degree roof and removing the athletics track, is still to come, targeted for completion before 2030.
How much has the renovation cost so far?
Around €34 million (about $39.5 million) invested in the completed first phase, with further spending planned for the second phase's roof and capacity expansion.
What makes Adrar Stadium's renovation different from Marrakech's?
Both are removing their athletics tracks, but Adrar Stadium is also adding a full 360-degree panoramic roof, a more extensive weather-protection upgrade than several of Morocco's other renovated venues.
When did Adrar Stadium open?
11 October 2013, with a friendly between Hassania Agadir and Algeria's JS Kabylie.
What club plays at Adrar Stadium?
Hassania Union Sport d'Agadir (HUSA), commonly known as Hassania Agadir, competing in Morocco's top division, the Botola Pro.
Has Hassania Agadir won any major trophies?
Not yet, but the club reached the 2019 Moroccan Throne Cup final and the CAF Confederation Cup quarter-finals in 2018-19, following up with a semi-final run in 2019-20.
Has Adrar Stadium hosted international tournaments before?
Yes, matches at the 2013 FIFA Club World Cup in its opening year, and matches at the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations following its first renovation phase.
How do I get to Adrar Stadium from central Agadir?
The stadium sits about 6km east of the city centre and 8-10km from the main beach hotel zone, roughly 15-20 minutes by taxi.
What's the nearest airport to Adrar Stadium?
Al Massira Airport (AGA) is about 20km southeast of the city, roughly 25-30 minutes from the beach zone by taxi.
Is Agadir far from Morocco's other host cities?
Yes, Agadir is Morocco's most geographically remote 2030 host city, roughly 1,200km from Tangier in the north and about 3 hours by road from Marrakech, worth factoring into multi-city travel plans.
Where should I stay for a match at Adrar Stadium?
Stay near the beach along La Corniche or in central Agadir rather than near the stadium itself. See our full Agadir guide for specific neighbourhood recommendations.
