Architecture & Design

A Facade Woven from Palm Leaves and Light

Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium sits within the wider Complexe Sportif Prince Moulay Abdellah in Rabat, a multi-venue complex that also includes a separate athletics stadium, the indoor Salle Moulay Abdellah arena, and an Olympic swimming pool. The football stadium itself was completely rebuilt by international sports-architecture firm Populous, working with Moroccan practice Orange Atelier, replacing the original 1983 stadium on the same site and reopening on 5 September 2025.

The design's most distinctive element is its parametric exterior facade, a pattern inspired by the woven palm leaves lining Rabat's boulevards and by traditional Moroccan craftsmanship such as "Point de Fez" embroidery. At night, the facade transforms entirely: LED pixels integrated directly into the surface turn the entire building into a programmable three-dimensional screen, using a lighting system in the same family as the technology behind Las Vegas's Sphere, with lighting design by the Lamalif Group.

"These aren't simple projections onto a surface: the surface itself is programmed and illuminated pixel by pixel, turning the stadium into a monumental 3D screen of its own."

François Clément, Senior Principal, Populous EMEA

Remarkably, the entire rebuild, from demolishing the old 1983 stadium to opening the new one, took under two years, a pace fast enough that it's been floated as a potential Guinness World Record for the quickest stadium construction on record. The roof covers three of the stadium's stands rather than the full bowl, a "windbreak" design paired with solar power generation, an advanced acoustic and sound system built for both football and large-scale concerts, and smart crowd-management technology throughout.

Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium is one of six Moroccan venues built or renovated for 2030, part of the same programme reshaping Ibn Batouta Stadium in Tangier and Grand Stade de Marrakech, though unlike those two, this one arrived as a complete rebuild rather than a renovation of the existing bowl.

Fan Experience

Already Tournament-Tested

Unlike several other Moroccan 2030 venues still mid-renovation, Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium has already been stress-tested at the highest level: it hosted both the opening match and the final of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations, a rare double for a single venue.

Get Stadium Updates

While AS FAR waited out construction playing home matches at Stade Municipal de Kénitra, the new stadium replaced a genuine piece of Moroccan football history: the original 1983 Stade Moulay Abdellah hosted matches at the 1988 AFCON, the 2014 and 2022 FIFA Club World Cups, the 2019 African Games, and was home to Africa's first Diamond League athletics meeting from 2016 before its 2023 demolition.

Matchday Atmosphere

Black, and Morocco's First Ultras

AS FAR traditionally plays in black, and its supporters hold a genuine claim to football history: Ultras Askary Rabat (UAR05), founded in 2005, is widely credited as the very first ultras group formed anywhere in Morocco.

Get Stadium Updates

A second group, Black Army (BA06), followed in 2006, and the pair have anchored AS FAR's home end ever since. As the club long associated with Morocco's Royal Armed Forces, AS FAR carries a distinct identity among Moroccan sides, sometimes nicknamed "the King's team," and its ultras culture, while sharing the tifos, flares and massed banners common across Moroccan football, has generally stayed clear of the political messaging associated with some of Casablanca's bigger supporter groups.

Since reopening, the stadium has taken on an extra layer of significance as Rabat's official fan zone during the 2026 World Cup in North America, packing in huge screening crowds through Morocco's run to the quarterfinals, where the Atlas Lions were eliminated 2-0 by France on 9 July 2026, a result that still leaves Morocco as one of a handful of nations to have reached the World Cup's final eight twice in the 2020s.

Street food outside the ground follows familiar Moroccan matchday patterns: grilled sandwiches, msemen flatbread, and sweet mint tea from vendors along the approach roads, alongside scarf and jersey sellers doing brisk business on any AS FAR or national-team matchday.

History & Significance

From National Stadium to Continental Stage

Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium has long served as Morocco's national stadium, hosting the country's biggest home fixtures for decades before its recent reconstruction replaced the original 1983 venue entirely.

All Morocco Stadiums

The rebuild forms part of the same national programme that renovated Ibn Batouta Stadium in Tangier, Grand Stade de Marrakech, Fez Grand Stadium and Adrar Stadium in Agadir, all commissioned to prepare Morocco's existing venues for AFCON 2025 and the 2030 World Cup. Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium is one of six Moroccan venues being built or renovated for 2030, alongside Hassan II Stadium in Casablanca.

The stadium is the home ground of Rabat's AS FAR (Association Sportive des Forces Armées Royales), founded in 1958, which became the first Moroccan club to win an international trophy when it lifted the 1985 African Cup of Champions Clubs. AS FAR's biggest domestic rivalry, sometimes called the Moroccan "Classico," runs against Wydad Casablanca, a rivalry dating back to a decisive 1959-60 league title playoff between the two clubs.

World Cup 2030 Role

The Capital's Flagship Venue

As Morocco's national stadium and its second-largest completed venue after Ibn Batouta Stadium in Tangier, Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium is a near-certain host for high-profile group and knockout matches, potentially including a 2030 semi-final.

Its rare distinction of hosting both the opening match and the final of AFCON 2025, within its first year back in operation, gives FIFA a fully proven, tournament-tested venue years ahead of kickoff, a track record few of Morocco's other stadiums can currently match. At 68,700 seats, the stadium comfortably clears FIFA's capacity requirements for all match stages up to and including semi-finals, positioning Rabat, Morocco's capital, as one of the tournament's most significant venues alongside Casablanca's Hassan II Stadium.

Its capacity sits close to Spain's Cívitas Metropolitano and Portugal's Estádio da Luz, both of which sit in a similar 65,000-70,000 bracket, a useful benchmark for fans planning a trip across all three host nations.

Getting There

From the Medina to the Complexe Sportif

Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium sits about 7km from Rabat's historic centre, within easy reach via the city's tram network and by road. As Morocco's capital and a stadium already used to hosting major tournament crowds, matchday transport infrastructure is more mature here than at several of Morocco's other venues.

Frequently Asked

Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium Questions

Who designed Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium?
International sports-architecture firm Populous, working with Moroccan practice Orange Atelier. The facade's parametric pattern was inspired by woven palm leaves and traditional "Point de Fez" embroidery, with lighting design by the Lamalif Group.
Is Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium a new stadium or a renovation?
It's a complete reconstruction on the site of the original 1983 stadium, part of the same national programme that renovated Ibn Batouta Stadium in Tangier, Grand Stade de Marrakech, Fez Grand Stadium and Adrar Stadium in Agadir.
Why does the facade light up at night?
LED pixels are integrated directly into the facade's woven pattern, turning the entire exterior into a programmable three-dimensional screen at night, a system in the same technology family as Las Vegas's Sphere.
What did FIFA say about the rebuilt stadium?
FIFA President Gianni Infantino called it a "jewel" during an official visit to inspect the redevelopment, a notably positive assessment for a venue that had only recently reopened.
What is the capacity of Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium?
68,700 seats, making it one of the largest stadiums in Morocco, second only to Hassan II Stadium once that new venue is complete. Some sources cite figures between roughly 68,095 and 69,500.
How long did the rebuild take?
Under two years, from the 2023 demolition of the original 1983 stadium to the September 2025 reopening, a pace fast enough to be floated as a potential Guinness World Record for the quickest stadium construction on record.
Is Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium ready for the World Cup?
Yes. Having already hosted the opening match and final of AFCON 2025, it's one of the most tournament-tested venues among Morocco's six 2030 host stadiums, with no further construction work outstanding.
Is Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium bigger than other Moroccan World Cup venues?
It's the second-largest completed Moroccan venue after Ibn Batouta Stadium in Tangier (75,500 seats), and will remain second overall once Hassan II Stadium (115,000 seats) opens in Casablanca.
What stadium did this replace?
The original Stade Moulay Abdellah, opened in 1983, which hosted the 1988 AFCON, FIFA Club World Cup finals in 2014 and 2022, the 2019 African Games, and Africa's first Diamond League athletics meeting from 2016, before its 2023 demolition.
What club plays at Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium?
AS FAR (Association Sportive des Forces Armées Royales), founded in 1958, which became the first Moroccan club to win an international trophy when it lifted the 1985 African Cup of Champions Clubs. It played home matches at Stade Municipal de Kénitra during the rebuild.
Does the Morocco national team play here?
Yes, as Morocco's rebuilt national stadium, it's the primary home venue for the Atlas Lions, Morocco's men's national football team, and served as Rabat's official fan zone during the 2026 World Cup.
Is there a big rivalry connected to this stadium?
Yes, AS FAR's biggest domestic rivalry, sometimes called the Moroccan "Classico," is against Wydad Casablanca, a rivalry dating back to a decisive 1959-60 league title playoff between the two clubs.
How do I get to Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium from central Rabat?
The stadium sits about 7km from Rabat's historic centre, reachable via the city's tram network and by road, roughly 15-20 minutes from the Medina or Ville Nouvelle.
What's the nearest airport to Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium?
Rabat-Salé Airport (RBA) is Rabat's main airport, a short drive from both the city centre and the stadium complex.
Where should I stay for a match at Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium?
Stay centrally in Rabat rather than near the stadium itself, since the surrounding area is a sports complex rather than a walkable neighbourhood. See our full Rabat guide for specific neighbourhood recommendations.
Can I visit Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium outside of matchdays?
As Morocco's active national stadium and a recently completed venue, no public tour programme has been officially announced as of this writing, though this is common at major stadiums once they're established.
