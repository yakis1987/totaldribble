Architecture & Design

An Ellipse Framed in Tile

Estadio do Dragao was designed by Portuguese architect Manuel Salgado, working with Jorge Estriga, Carlos Cruz and Ines Cruz, to replace FC Porto's previous ground, Estadio das Antas, ahead of Euro 2004. Construction began in late 2001 but was briefly halted in February 2002 after a dispute between Porto's mayor and the club over the surrounding estate plan, only resuming once a consensus was reached.

The stadium's elliptical form was deliberately designed to integrate with the surrounding green spaces and improve urban access, its structure built from 84 radial reinforced concrete frames connected by horizontal circumferential beams. The steel roof spans the entire seating bowl via two lattice girders stretching 178 metres, with heights ranging from 5.25 to 10.80 metres.

A distinctive local touch sets Dragao apart from Portugal's other 2030 venues: its frame is wrapped in 21,000 square metres of azulejos, the traditional Portuguese and Spanish painted tin-glazed ceramic tilework, giving the stadium a genuinely regional architectural identity.

Modernization & Fan Experience

The First Overhaul Since 2003

FC Porto announced a comprehensive modernization programme in June 2025, the stadium's first major overhaul since opening, with work beginning on 20 June 2025. The project focuses on new hospitality areas, advanced lighting systems, expanded kitchen facilities, and broader operational upgrades, phased carefully across the 2025-26 and 2026-27 pre-seasons to minimise disruption to matches.

What We Don't Know Yet

A total project budget and any change to the stadium's 50,033-seat capacity haven't been officially confirmed, and the roof replacement remains only under study rather than committed. We'll update this page once further details are announced.

History & Significance

A Teenage Messi's First Bow

Estadio do Dragao's inauguration on 16 November 2003, a friendly against Barcelona, is remembered for more than Porto's 2-0 win: it marked the competitive debut of a 16-year-old Lionel Messi for the visiting Catalan side. The match drew a then-record attendance of 52,004, though turf problems soon after forced Porto to temporarily return to the old Estadio das Antas until the pitch was replanted by mid-February 2004.

Estadio do Dragao is one of three confirmed Portuguese venues for the 2030 World Cup, alongside Estadio da Luz and Estadio Jose Alvalade, both in Lisbon.

The stadium is home to Porto's FC Porto, one of Portugal's "Big Three" clubs. Its name, chosen in May 2003 after internal debate over alternatives including club legends' names, honours the dragon on both FC Porto's crest and the city's own coat of arms, symbolising strength and vitality. Beyond football, the stadium has hosted concerts by the Rolling Stones, Coldplay, Muse and One Direction.

World Cup 2030 Role

The North's World Cup Anchor

As the only 2030 World Cup venue outside Lisbon among Portugal's three stadiums, Estadio do Dragao gives the tournament genuine geographic reach across the country, tentatively scheduled to host matches up to the quarter-final stage. At 50,033 seats, it meets FIFA's group-stage and knockout requirements comfortably.

Porto's own recent hosting record, including the 2021 Champions League final and the inaugural 2019 UEFA Nations League Final, gives organisers confidence the venue and city can handle major tournament crowds and media attention well ahead of 2030.

Getting There

Its Own Metro Station

Estadio do Dragao has its own dedicated Metro station, Estadio do Dragao, served by Lines A, B, E and F, connecting directly to Porto's city centre and Francisco Sa Carneiro Airport.

Frequently Asked

Estadio do Dragao Questions

Who designed Estadio do Dragao?
Portuguese architect Manuel Salgado, working with Jorge Estriga, Carlos Cruz and Ines Cruz, designed the stadium ahead of its 2003 opening.
What's distinctive about the stadium's architecture?
An elliptical form integrated with surrounding green spaces, 84 radial reinforced concrete frames, and a steel roof spanning the seating bowl on two 178-metre lattice girders. Its frame is wrapped in 21,000 square metres of traditional Portuguese azulejo tilework.
Why is it called "Estadio do Dragao"?
"Dragon Stadium" in English, chosen in May 2003 to honour the dragon on FC Porto's crest and the city's coat of arms, symbolising strength and vitality.
How much did the stadium cost to build?
Approximately €125 million, with roughly €18.5 million subsidised by the Portuguese government ahead of Euro 2004.
What is the capacity of Estadio do Dragao?
50,033 seats, the third-largest stadium in Portugal.
What does the current modernization involve?
New hospitality areas, advanced lighting systems, a central kitchen serving the whole stadium, AI-powered self-service bars, and a move to fully digital ticketing, phased across the 2025-26 and 2026-27 pre-seasons.
Is the roof being replaced?
The roof has never been replaced since the stadium opened in 2003. FC Porto has launched studies into its condition and is considering a replacement strategy, though nothing has been formally committed yet.
When did the modernization begin?
Work began on 20 June 2025, the stadium's first major overhaul since it opened in 2003.
When did Estadio do Dragao open?
16 November 2003, with a friendly against Barcelona, Porto winning 2-0. The match is best remembered for a 16-year-old Lionel Messi's competitive debut for the visiting Catalan side.
Has Estadio do Dragao hosted major finals?
Yes, the 2020-21 UEFA Champions League final and the inaugural 2019 UEFA Nations League Final, which Portugal won at home.
What club plays at Estadio do Dragao?
FC Porto, one of Portugal's "Big Three" clubs alongside Benfica and Sporting CP.
What major concerts has the stadium hosted?
The Rolling Stones, Coldplay, Muse and One Direction have all performed at Estadio do Dragao.
How do I get to Estadio do Dragao?
The stadium has its own Metro station, served by Lines A, B, E and F, roughly 15-20 minutes from central Porto.
What's the nearest airport to Estadio do Dragao?
Francisco Sa Carneiro Airport (OPO) connects directly via Metro, roughly 25-30 minutes from the stadium.
Where should I stay for a match at Estadio do Dragao?
Central Porto neighbourhoods like Aliados or Ribeira offer easy Metro access. See our full Porto guide for specific recommendations.
Is Estadio do Dragao Portugal's only 2030 venue outside Lisbon?
Yes, it's the only one of Portugal's three confirmed World Cup stadiums located outside the capital, giving the tournament a genuine northern Portuguese presence.
