Santiago Bernabéu

Architecture & Design

A Brutalist Bowl, Wrapped in Steel

Santiago Bernabéu Stadium first opened in 1947, designed by Spanish architects Manuel Muñoz Monasterio and Luis Alemany Soler. In 2014, an international design competition to reimagine the stadium was won by German firm gmp Architekten (Gerkan, Marg and Partners) working alongside Spanish studios L35 Arquitectos and Ribas & Ribas Arquitectos, beating out submissions from Foster + Partners, Rafael Moneo, Herzog & de Meuron and Populous.

Construction ran from 2019 to late 2024, transforming the stadium from the outside in without ever fully closing it to matches. The most visible change is the facade: the original Brutalist concrete structure is now wrapped in roughly 7,500 V-shaped stainless steel slats, which enable outdoor ventilation while giving the stadium a shimmering, futuristic exterior that can project images via an integrated LED lighting system.

Unlike Barcelona's Camp Nou, which closed entirely for its own renovation, or Morocco's ground-up new builds like Hassan II Stadium and the rebuilt Ibn Batouta Stadium, Real Madrid's approach kept the ground playable throughout, a genuinely unusual feat of sequencing that added complexity and cost but preserved continuous matchday revenue across the five-year build. Madrid's other 2030 venue, Atlético Madrid's Cívitas Metropolitano, took a different route again: a purpose-built football ground converted from a former athletics stadium shell rather than adapted piece by piece around live matches.

Inside, the renovation reworked concourse circulation almost as thoroughly as the exterior, widening walkways, adding new vertical cores, and improving sightlines from steepened lower tiers, changes less visible in photographs than the facade but arguably more consequential for the actual matchday experience of getting to a seat.

The steel-slat facade concept has since become something of a reference point for other stadium projects chasing a similarly futuristic, media-capable exterior, though few have matched its scale: Portugal's Estádio da Luz has floated its own expansion and modernisation plans partly in response to the bar Madrid's renovation set for what a 2030 host venue should look like on matchday broadcasts.

Real Madrid's total financing for the project reached approximately €1.17 billion, drawn across three separate loans between 2019 and 2023, with annual repayments running through 2049.

Engineering & Fan Experience

A Stadium That Disappears Underground

The renovation's signature engineering feat is the Hypogea retractable pitch system, developed by Sener: six giant steel trays, roughly 9,000 tonnes in total, that lower the natural grass surface into a climate-controlled underground chamber in under five hours.

The multifunctional design has already been tested well beyond football: Taylor Swift became the first international artist to play the renovated stadium in May 2024, Karol G holds the record for most concert performances there, and the venue has also hosted NFL regular-season games, a detail FIFA officials have reportedly cited approvingly as evidence of the stadium's operational reliability at global scale. The retractable pitch and roof are what make the venue viable as a year-round entertainment complex rather than a football-only ground.

What We Don't Know Yet

The stadium's exact seating capacity remains genuinely disputed, see the Capacity & Construction FAQ tab below for the full picture. Real Madrid has not published an official figure, and estimates from FIFA's own bid documents, La Liga, and the club itself all differ.

Matchday Atmosphere

Hala Madrid and the Grada Fans

Real Madrid's matchday soundtrack centres on "Hala Madrid," a stirring club anthem sung before, during and after every home game, alongside call-and-response chants like "¡Así, así, así gana el Madrid!"

The south stand is home to Grada Fans RMCF, the club's official organised supporters' group since 2014, formed after Real Madrid severed ties with its previous fan group over years of behavioural and disciplinary problems and set out to build a louder, younger, family-friendly matchday section instead. Beyond that curated core, dozens of independent peñas (fan clubs) from across Spain and abroad travel in for big fixtures, part of a broader Madridista culture that treats attending a match at the Bernabéu as something close to a pilgrimage.

El Clásico against Barcelona remains the fixture that defines the calendar, regularly among the most-watched club matches on the planet, with average Real Madrid broadcasts drawing more than 37 million television viewers worldwide even outside that rivalry. Champions League nights carry their own distinct intensity, with the stadium's acoustics, redesigned during the renovation, noticeably amplifying crowd noise compared to the pre-2019 bowl.

Matchday food inside the renovated stadium runs toward the modern and upscale end of the spectrum: sit-down restaurants, branded concession stands and a members-only skywalk bar with panoramic Madrid views, a different register from the street-vendor culture found around several other 2030 host venues.

History & Significance

Nearly Eight Decades at the Heart of Madrid

Santiago Bernabéu Stadium opened in 1947, named after the former Real Madrid player and club president whose vision drove its construction, and has hosted the 1982 World Cup final, multiple European Cup and Champions League finals, and decades of Real Madrid's biggest nights.

Expanded to around 125,000 in a 1955 renovation and later reduced as safety standards evolved, the stadium's story since has been one of gradual modernisation punctuated by two genuinely transformative moments: the 1982 World Cup final, and the 2019-2024 renovation. Santiago Bernabéu Stadium is a leading candidate to host the 2030 World Cup final on 21 July, alongside Camp Nou in Barcelona and Hassan II Stadium in Casablanca.

World Cup 2030 Role

The Favourite for the Final

Reporting through mid-2026 consistently points to Santiago Bernabéu as the frontrunner to host the 2030 World Cup final on 21 July, helped by its brand-new renovation, central Madrid location, and what multiple reports describe as a strong working relationship between Real Madrid president Florentino Pérez and FIFA president Gianni Infantino.

See Spain Guide All World Cup Stadiums

Nothing is officially confirmed. FIFA's full host-stadium list is expected only in December 2026, and both Barcelona's Camp Nou and Morocco's Hassan II Stadium remain genuine competitors. Camp Nou's own renovation targets roughly 105,000 seats, which would make it the largest stadium in Europe and a hard capacity argument to ignore, while Hassan II is being built for 115,000 with the explicit stated goal of hosting the final. Portugal's Estádio da Luz has even been floated by some as a neutral compromise between Spain and Morocco, contingent on Benfica's own planned expansion to around 83,000 seats.

One complication for Madrid's bid: FIFA requires a minimum capacity of roughly 80,000 for the final, and the Bernabéu's own bid-book figure of 78,297 sits just under that threshold, one reason reports suggest Real Madrid is exploring adding roughly 1,700 further seats to put the question beyond doubt. Spanish federation president Rafael Louzán has publicly argued the final "has" to be in Spain given the country's leading role in the joint bid, a sign of how politically charged the decision has become even before FIFA weighs in.

Frequently Asked

Santiago Bernabéu Questions

Who designed the Santiago Bernabéu renovation?
German firm gmp Architekten (Gerkan, Marg and Partners), working with Spanish studios L35 Arquitectos and Ribas & Ribas Arquitectos, won the 2014 design competition, beating Foster + Partners, Rafael Moneo, Herzog & de Meuron and Populous.
What is the stadium's steel facade made from?
Roughly 7,500 V-shaped stainless steel slats wrap the original Brutalist concrete structure, allowing outdoor ventilation while giving the stadium a shimmering exterior with an integrated LED lighting system.
Did Real Madrid ever fully close the stadium during renovation?
No. Construction ran from 2019 to late 2024, transforming the stadium from the outside in while Real Madrid continued playing home matches there throughout.
How did Real Madrid finance the renovation?
Through approximately €1.17 billion in total financing, drawn across three separate loans taken out between 2019 and 2023, with annual repayments running through 2049.
What is the actual capacity of Santiago Bernabéu?
Genuinely disputed. FIFA's own bid documents cite 78,297, La Liga has stated figures between 83,186 and 84,000, and Real Madrid president Florentino Pérez has said "80,000, give or take 1,000." The highest recorded league attendance so far is 78,107. We use approximately 83,000 as a working estimate.
Why won't Real Madrid confirm the exact capacity?
The club has not explained the discrepancy publicly. Structural changes during the renovation, including new folding seats of different dimensions, make it genuinely difficult for outside observers to verify a precise figure without official confirmation.
Is Real Madrid planning to expand the stadium further?
Reports suggest the club is exploring expansion toward roughly 85,000 seats, specifically to strengthen its bid to host the 2030 World Cup final, which requires a minimum capacity around 80,000, above the stadium's 78,297-seat bid-book figure.
How does Santiago Bernabéu's capacity compare to its World Cup final rivals?
It's smaller than both leading rivals: Barcelona's renovated Camp Nou targets around 105,000 seats, and Morocco's Hassan II Stadium is being built for 115,000, both comfortably clearing FIFA's roughly 80,000-seat final requirement without any capacity debate.
When did Santiago Bernabéu open?
1947, named after former Real Madrid player and club president Santiago Bernabéu (1895-1978), whose vision drove its construction.
Has Santiago Bernabéu hosted a World Cup final before?
Yes, the 1982 FIFA World Cup final, in which Italy defeated West Germany, part of Spain's hosting of the 1982 tournament.
What club plays at Santiago Bernabéu?
Real Madrid CF, one of the most decorated clubs in world football history, with matches averaging over 37 million television viewers.
Has the renovated stadium hosted concerts and other events?
Yes, Taylor Swift became the first international artist to play the renovated stadium in May 2024, Karol G holds the record for most concert performances there, and the venue has also hosted NFL regular-season games.
How do I get to Santiago Bernabéu?
The stadium has its own Metro station, Santiago Bernabéu, on Line 10, making it one of the most transit-accessible stadiums in Spain, roughly 15-20 minutes from central Madrid.
What's the nearest airport to Santiago Bernabéu?
Adolfo Suárez Madrid-Barajas Airport (MAD), Spain's largest hub, roughly 30-40 minutes from the stadium via Metro and taxi.
Where should I stay for a match at Santiago Bernabéu?
Anywhere in central Madrid works well thanks to the stadium's own Metro stop. See our full Madrid guide for specific neighbourhood recommendations.
Can I tour Santiago Bernabéu outside of matchdays?
Real Madrid offers regular stadium tours covering the museum, changing rooms and pitchside areas, though tour availability and pricing should be checked directly with the club closer to your visit.
