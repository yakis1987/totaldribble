Architecture & Design

A Stadium Named for a Native Explorer

Ibn Batouta Stadium, officially the Grand Stade de Tanger, was designed by JK Architecture under chief architect Jaouad Khattabi and built between 2002 and its April 2011 opening. It takes its name from Ibn Battuta, the 14th-century Tangier-born explorer whose decades of travel across Africa, Asia and the Middle East made him one of history's most famous travellers. His tomb remains a small landmark in Tangier's old Medina, giving the stadium a direct link to the city's identity as a gateway between continents.

The stadium's transformation into its current form happened in two distinct phases. A first expansion, running 2020-2023, extended the curved end stands to match the height of the side stands, raising capacity from 45,000 to 65,000. A second, far more radical phase, designed by Cabinet d'Architecture Amaoui Anouar Guériri and beginning in summer 2023, removed the athletics track entirely, lowered the pitch, added a new lower tier of seating close to the field, and wrapped the whole venue in full roof coverage and a new decorative facade.

That second phase reopened the stadium on 14 November 2025, with Morocco beating Mozambique 1-0 in the inaugural friendly, just weeks before the venue hosted matches at AFCON 2025. The stadium anchors a 74-hectare Sports City on Tangier's southern edge, a dedicated sporting district managed by SONARGES and built around the venue rather than integrated into the older parts of the city.

Fan Experience

Africa's First Hybrid Pitch

The renovation's most distinctive technical feature is its playing surface: a hybrid natural grass and synthetic fibre pitch, reportedly the first of its kind in Africa, engineered to improve drainage and player safety across a heavier fixture schedule.

Since its original 2011 opening, the stadium has hosted the 2011 and 2017 editions of the Trophée des Champions (the French Super Cup), the 2018 Supercopa de España, matches at the 2018 African Nations Championship, and the 2022 FIFA Club World Cup, giving it a well-established international events pedigree well before the current renovation. Its reopening for AFCON 2025 tested the new capacity and facilities under genuine tournament conditions years ahead of the World Cup.

What We Don't Know Yet

Precise seating-tier breakdowns and detailed hospitality specifications haven't been published with the same granularity as Hassan II Stadium's, and reported capacity figures vary slightly across sources, from 75,500 to 75,600 or occasionally higher. Matchday Atmosphere

Blue, White and Ultras Hercules

IR Tanger plays in blue and white, backed by Ultras Hercules (also known as Ultras Tanger 2003), founded in 2003 and one of the longer-running organised supporter groups in Moroccan football.

Get Stadium Updates

Ultras Hercules stage regular tifos and choreography for IR Tanger's home fixtures, and have been especially vocal around the club's derby with Moghreb Tétouan, a fixture with an almost perfectly even head-to-head record that carries outsized weight across the Tangier-Tétouan-Al Hoceima region. The rebuilt stadium already has one full-house atmosphere on record to point to: a 26 March 2023 friendly in which Morocco beat Brazil 2-1 in front of a capacity crowd, played while the venue still sat at its intermediate 65,000-seat configuration.

Tangier's position facing the Strait of Gibraltar adds a genuinely unusual ingredient to matchday: fans can combine a trip here with a same-day crossing to Spain, and the city's own football culture carries a distinct northern-Morocco identity separate from the Casablanca-Rabat rivalries that dominate national attention.

Street food outside the ground follows familiar Moroccan matchday patterns, grilled sandwiches, msemen flatbread and sweet mint tea from vendors along the Sports City approach, alongside the stadium's own concourse kiosks.

History & Significance

From 2011 Opening to 2025 Rebirth

Ibn Batouta Stadium opened in April 2011 and quickly built an international events resume well beyond domestic football, a track record that made it a natural candidate for a major renovation once Morocco's 2030 World Cup bid gained momentum.

All Morocco Stadiums

The inaugural match, on 26 April 2011, paired IR Tanger against Atlético Madrid's B team, followed by Raja CA against Atlético Madrid's first team. Three months later the stadium hosted its first Trophée des Champions, with Marseille beating Lille 5-4. Morocco was originally slated to host the 2015 AFCON here too, before FIFA and CAF stripped the country of hosting rights over Ebola-related health concerns, a rare setback in the venue's otherwise steady rise in profile.

Ibn Batouta Stadium is one of six Moroccan venues being built or renovated for 2030, alongside Hassan II Stadium in Casablanca and Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, both part of the same national upgrade programme.

The stadium is the home ground of Tangier's Ittihad Riadi de Tanger (IR Tanger), founded in 1957 and playing in blue and white, first-time Botola Pro champions in 2017-18. In November 2025, IR Tanger signed a three-year strategic partnership with Eagle Football Group, the multi-club ownership group led by John Textor that also holds stakes in Olympique Lyonnais, Botafogo and Crystal Palace, timed almost exactly with the stadium's own reopening.

World Cup 2030 Role

Morocco's Largest Ready Venue

At 75,500 seats, Ibn Batouta Stadium is Morocco's largest completed World Cup venue, and will remain the country's second-largest overall once Hassan II Stadium opens in Casablanca at 115,000. Its head start matters: while several Moroccan venues are still mid-renovation, this one has already been open, tested and hosting major tournament football since November 2025.

Tangier's position on the Strait of Gibraltar, just 14km from Spain, adds a unique cross-continental dimension: it's the only Moroccan host city with a direct ferry link to a Spanish World Cup host region, letting fans plausibly attend matches in both countries within the same trip. Some reporting has even floated the possibility of a semi-final at Ibn Batouta Stadium, though FIFA has not confirmed match assignments for individual venues.

Its capacity sits in a similar bracket to Spain's Santiago Bernabéu and Portugal's Estádio da Luz, both comfortably above 65,000 seats, a useful benchmark for fans planning a trip that spans all three host nations.

Frequently Asked

Ibn Batouta Stadium Questions

Who is Ibn Batouta Stadium named after?
Ibn Battuta, the 14th-century Tangier-born explorer whose decades of travel across Africa, Asia and the Middle East made him one of history's most famous travellers. His tomb is a small landmark in Tangier's old Medina.
Who designed the stadium?
JK Architecture, under chief architect Jaouad Khattabi, designed the original 2002-2011 stadium. The 2023-2025 football-specific renovation was designed by Cabinet d'Architecture Amaoui Anouar Guériri.
What changed in the stadium's renovation?
Two phases: first, raising the curved end stands (2020-2023, capacity to 65,000); then removing the athletics track, lowering the pitch, adding a lower tier, full roof coverage, 142 private boxes, and a hybrid natural-synthetic pitch reportedly the first of its kind in Africa (2023-2025, capacity to 75,500).
What is the Sports City around the stadium?
A dedicated 74-hectare sporting district on Tangier's southern edge, managed by SONARGES and built around the stadium rather than integrated into the older parts of the city.
What is the capacity of Ibn Batouta Stadium?
75,500 seats following the November 2025 renovation, up from 45,000 originally, making it Morocco's largest completed World Cup venue. Some sources cite 75,600 or slightly higher.
What is the hybrid pitch?
It's a hybrid natural grass and synthetic fibre surface, reportedly the first of its kind in Africa, designed to improve drainage and player safety across a heavier fixture schedule.
Has the stadium already hosted major matches?
Yes, extensively: the 2011 and 2017 editions of the Trophée des Champions, the 2018 Supercopa de España, the 2018 African Nations Championship, the 2022 FIFA Club World Cup, a full-capacity Morocco 2-1 Brazil friendly in March 2023, and matches at the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations.
Was Ibn Batouta Stadium ever meant to host AFCON earlier?
Yes, it was a confirmed venue for the 2015 AFCON, but Morocco was stripped of hosting rights over Ebola-related health concerns before the tournament, and the event was relocated to Equatorial Guinea.
When did Ibn Batouta Stadium open?
The original stadium opened in April 2011. Its second, more transformative renovation phase reopened the venue on 14 November 2025 at its current 75,500-seat capacity.
What club plays at Ibn Batouta Stadium?
Ittihad Riadi de Tanger (IR Tanger), founded in 1957, competing in Morocco's top division, the Botola Pro, and playing in blue and white. The club won its first Botola Pro title in 2017-18.
What is the latest news involving IR Tanger?
In November 2025, IR Tanger signed a three-year strategic partnership with Eagle Football Group, the multi-club ownership group led by John Textor that also holds stakes in Olympique Lyonnais, Botafogo and Crystal Palace.
Who are IR Tanger's main rivals?
Moghreb Tétouan, in a fixture known regionally for its near-perfectly even head-to-head record, one of the most closely contested derbies in northern Moroccan football.
How do I get to Ibn Batouta Stadium from central Tangier?
The stadium sits within a Sports City on the city's southern edge, roughly 15-20 minutes by taxi from the Medina or Ville Nouvelle.
Can I combine a match here with a trip to Spain?
Yes, Tangier is the only Moroccan host city with a direct ferry link to Spain, roughly 35-40 minutes to Tarifa, making it possible to attend matches in both countries within the same trip.
What's the nearest airport to Ibn Batouta Stadium?
Ibn Battouta Airport (TNG) sits about 15km southwest of the city, roughly 15-20 minutes from the centre by taxi.
Where should I stay for a match at Ibn Batouta Stadium?
Stay centrally in Tangier, in the Medina, Kasbah or Ville Nouvelle, rather than near the stadium itself. See our full Tangier guide for specific neighbourhood recommendations.
