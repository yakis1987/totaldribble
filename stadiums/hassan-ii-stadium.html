Architecture & Design

A Tent, Reimagined at National Scale

In March 2024, a consortium led by Populous and Casablanca and Paris-based studio Oualalou + Choi won the international design competition for the Grand Stade Hassan II, beating submissions from Cruz y Ortiz, Foster + Partners, GMP Architects, Herzog & de Meuron, HPP Architects and Zaha Hadid Architects. Populous handled the sports-venue engineering side of the design, working alongside Tarik Oualalou, the project's lead architect and founding partner of Oualalou + Choi.

The concept draws directly on the moussem, a traditional Moroccan communal gathering historically held beneath vast tents. Tarik Oualalou describes the design as rooted in three "ancient and primordial figures": the moussem itself, the tent, and the garden, layered onto the topography and landscapes of Morocco. The result is a stadium shaped like a colossal tent settled into the El Mansouria forest, its silhouette described by Populous's Christopher Lee as having a "mountain-like profile."

The defining architectural gesture is the roof: a translucent aluminium lattice that filters natural light into the bowl below while casting a distinctive silhouette over the landscape from outside. A structural ring of 32 monumental stair towers supports the roof and doubles as a series of scenographic entrance gateways into the stadium. In June 2026, the project won the Jury Prize in the "Unbuilt Sports & Recreation" category at the Architizer A+Awards, a notable honour for a stadium still under construction.

"It is rooted in ancient and primordial figures: the moussem, the tent, and the garden, as well as the topography and landscapes of Morocco. It's a generous space, open to the world and respectful to the nature it protects."

Tarik Oualalou, Lead Architect, Oualalou + Choi

Gardens run through the design at two levels: elevated platforms around the 32 stair towers host lush greenery roughly 28 metres above ground, while a further series of botanical gardens sits at ground level beneath the outer reaches of the roof canopy. Populous describes the overall effect as "an oasis of greenery under the translucent roof canopy," extending the stadium's public realm well beyond the seating bowl itself. By design, the bowl itself rises in three tiers: architect Tarik Oualalou has described roughly 22,500 seats on the first level, 40,000-42,000 on the second, and a third ring completing the 115,000 total.

Hassan II Stadium is the largest and most ambitious of six Moroccan venues built or renovated for 2030, alongside Grand Stade de Marrakech, Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, and Ibn Batouta Stadium in Tangier, which has already reopened after its own major expansion.

Engineering & Fan Experience

Built for Verticality and Density

Populous's stated goal was to combine the monumentality of the world's great stadiums with the emotional intensity of the most passionate football arenas, using verticality and density to bring spectators as close to the pitch as the bowl's scale allows.

Construction is organised into twelve procurement lots, three of which had been awarded by March 2026: earthworks (356 million MAD, won by SGTM), technical installations and fluids (763 million MAD), and the roof and facade (3.7 billion MAD, won by a joint venture of SGTM and TGCC). As of May 2026, project officials told Reuters the stadium was roughly 30% complete after nine months of the main structural phase, with 5,000 workers on site scaling toward 10,000 at peak; a separate report by Spanish newspaper AS the same month put progress closer to 40%.

One argument Tarik Oualalou has made directly to FIFA: because the stadium is being designed from scratch rather than retrofitted, it can build in dedicated FIFA offices and operational space from day one, something older, already-built stadiums like the Bernabéu can't easily replicate. The wider 100-hectare (some reports say up to 150-hectare) complex also includes 70,000m² of training pitches and an energy centre, a 38,000m² athletics stadium, a 34,200m² aquatics centre, and further gymnastics and multi-sport halls.

What We Don't Know Yet

Because Hassan II Stadium is still under construction, several details remain unsettled. Reports put site size at anywhere from 100 to 150 hectares. By March 2026, roughly 7.45 billion MAD had already been committed across just three of twelve procurement lots, already exceeding the original 5.1 billion MAD budget figure, and a final total hasn't been confirmed. Precise pitch-to-first-row distances and formal acoustic figures also haven't been published. We'll update this page as soon as that data becomes public, rather than guess at numbers.

Matchday Atmosphere

Africa's Fiercest Derby is Coming Here

Hassan II Stadium hasn't hosted a match yet, but the atmosphere it inherits is already legendary: Casablanca's Raja CA and Wydad AC currently share Stade Mohammed V for one of Africa's most passionate derbies, and both clubs are expected to relocate here once construction finishes.

Get Construction Updates

Raja's support is anchored by two groups: Ultras Green Boys 2005, widely credited as the first organised ultras movement in Morocco, and Ultras Eagles 2006, which split off the following year. Both occupy the south "Magana" end at Mohammed V, wearing Raja's green and white, colours chosen to represent hope and the city of Casablanca. Across the pitch, Wydad's "Winners," formed in 2005, pack the north curve some 10,000 strong, staging choreographed card mosaics and torch displays under the motto "Free souls." Both groups lean toward vocal, sometimes politically charged chanting, a well-documented feature of Moroccan ultras culture generally, though this page keeps its focus on the football and matchday spectacle rather than that wider context.

Once Hassan II Stadium opens, the Casablanca Derby, one of the fixtures most associated with Moroccan football, is expected to move here permanently, giving both sets of ultras a stage built for genuinely extreme scale: each end of the new stadium alone holds roughly 29,500 fans, nearly three times the Winners' current turnout at Mohammed V.

Street food traditions from Mohammed V, grilled sandwiches, msemen flatbread, and sweet mint tea from vendors lining the approach streets, are expected to transfer with the clubs, alongside new fixed-price concourse kiosks built into the stadium itself.

History & Significance

A Stadium Twenty Years in the Making

A grand new stadium for Casablanca has been proposed and shelved twice before, first for the 2010 World Cup bid, then again for 2026 with a 93,000-seat Cruz y Ortiz Arquitectos design that never left paper. The current project is the one that finally broke ground.

All Morocco Stadiums

A grand new stadium for Casablanca was first floated for Morocco's bid to host the 2010 World Cup, which the country lost to South Africa by a narrow 14-10 vote. The idea resurfaced for the 2026 bid, which included a 93,000-seat design by Spanish firm Cruz y Ortiz Arquitectos intended to host the opening match and final, but that bid lost to the United 2026 bid (USA, Canada, Mexico) in 2018. The project only gained real momentum after Morocco's historic run to the semi-finals of the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, which strengthened the country's football federation to push hard for a fresh World Cup bid.

World Cup 2030 Role

A Genuine Final Contender

Hassan II Stadium was conceived from the outset with the explicit goal of hosting the 2030 World Cup final, and its scale alone makes it a serious contender: at 115,000 seats it would be the largest stadium used at any World Cup in the modern era.

FIFA has not yet confirmed the final's venue, and reporting through 2026 has also floated Spain's Santiago Bernabéu and Barcelona's Camp Nou as candidates, so nothing is settled. One argument Moroccan officials have made to Spanish media: roughly 9,000 VIP and VVIP seats, an unusually high figure even against the Bernabéu's own renovated hospitality offering, alongside dedicated office space designed into the building specifically for FIFA's operational needs.

Whichever match it ultimately hosts, the stadium is designed to be fully FIFA-compliant for top-tier international competition, with construction timed to leave buffer room for test events and certification well before the tournament begins on 8 June 2030. At more than double the capacity of Portugal's largest venue, Estádio da Luz (65,000 seats), Hassan II Stadium sits in a class of its own among the tournament's 21 confirmed venues.

Getting There

From Casablanca to El Mansouria

Hassan II Stadium sits roughly 38-40km north of central Casablanca, in Benslimane Province, well outside the city itself. Authorities are upgrading highways, rail links and public transport connections specifically to support matchday access, though a dedicated train station and finalised shuttle routes haven't been confirmed as of this writing.

Frequently Asked

Hassan II Stadium Questions

Who designed Hassan II Stadium?
A consortium led by global sports architecture firm Populous and Paris/Casablanca-based studio Oualalou + Choi, led by Tarik Oualalou. They won an international design competition in March 2024, beating firms including Zaha Hadid Architects, Foster + Partners and Herzog & de Meuron.
Why is Hassan II Stadium shaped like a tent?
The design draws on the moussem, a traditional Moroccan communal gathering historically held under large tents. The stadium's roof and overall form reinterpret that cultural tradition at a national, monumental scale.
What is the Hassan II Stadium roof made from?
A unique translucent aluminium lattice, supported by a structural ring of 32 monumental stair towers. The roof shades both the interior bowl and the outdoor botanical gardens beneath its outer edges, while still filtering natural light through.
Has Hassan II Stadium won any awards?
Yes, it won the Jury Prize in the "Unbuilt Sports & Recreation" category at the Architizer A+Awards 2026, a notable recognition for a stadium that hasn't been completed yet.
What is the capacity of Hassan II Stadium?
115,000 seats, which would make it the largest football stadium in the world once complete, surpassing North Korea's Rungrado 1st of May Stadium among purpose-built football venues.
How far along is construction?
Reports from May 2026 put progress at roughly 30-40%, with three of twelve procurement lots awarded (earthworks, technical installations, and roof/facade) and 5,000 workers on site, scaling toward 10,000 at peak.
When will Hassan II Stadium be finished?
Officials have targeted December 2027, leaving roughly two and a half years for test events and certification before the 2030 World Cup kicks off on 8 June 2030.
How much did Hassan II Stadium cost to build?
The project's budget was originally set at 5.1 billion Moroccan dirhams, financed through an October 2023 agreement between the Moroccan government and the Caisse de Dépôt et de Gestion, though committed spending had already surpassed that figure by early 2026, with a final total not yet confirmed.
Why wasn't Hassan II Stadium built for the 2010 or 2026 World Cup bids?
A grand Casablanca stadium was proposed for both bids, including a 93,000-seat Cruz y Ortiz Arquitectos design for 2026, but Morocco lost the 2010 vote to South Africa by a narrow 14-10 margin and lost the 2026 vote to the United bid of the USA, Canada and Mexico in 2018. The project only gained real momentum after Morocco's run to the 2022 World Cup semi-finals.
What is Stade Mohammed V, and how does it relate to Hassan II Stadium?
Stade Mohammed V is Casablanca's existing, smaller stadium, currently shared by Raja CA and Wydad AC for the Casablanca Derby. Once Hassan II Stadium opens, it is expected to take over as the shared home for both clubs.
Will Hassan II Stadium host the World Cup final?
It's a genuine contender given its record-breaking size and was designed with that explicit goal in mind, but FIFA has not confirmed the final's venue. Spain's Santiago Bernabéu and Camp Nou have also been floated as candidates. A decision is expected closer to the tournament.
Which clubs will play at Hassan II Stadium?
After the World Cup, it is expected to become the shared home of Casablanca's two biggest clubs, Raja Club Athletic and Wydad Athletic Club, who currently share the older, smaller Stade Mohammed V.
How do I get to Hassan II Stadium from Casablanca?
The stadium sits about 38-40km north of central Casablanca in Benslimane Province, roughly 40-50 minutes by road. Authorities are upgrading highways and transport links specifically for matchday access, though final shuttle and public transport plans haven't been confirmed yet.
Is there a train station at Hassan II Stadium?
Not currently. Authorities have discussed rail and public transport upgrades to serve the stadium, but a dedicated station and confirmed routes have not been announced as of this writing. We'll update this page once official plans are released.
Where should I stay for a match at Hassan II Stadium?
Stay centrally in Casablanca rather than near the stadium itself, since it sits well outside the city with no walkable neighbourhood nearby. See our full Casablanca guide for specific neighbourhood recommendations.
Can I visit Hassan II Stadium before it's finished?
As an active construction site, Hassan II Stadium is not open for public tours at this stage. Once complete, stadium tours are common at major venues, though none have been officially announced yet for this one.
